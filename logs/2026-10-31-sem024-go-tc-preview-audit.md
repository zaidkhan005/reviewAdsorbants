## Target scope — do not pool the three drugs
The study evaluates:
- Tetracycline (TC);
- Oxytetracycline (OTC);
- Doxycycline (DOX).

TC is the target relevant to the semester paper. OTC and DOX are separate analytes; the publisher abstract says their adsorption isotherms were discussed and compared. It does not expose numerical OTC/DOX capacities or show that the three drugs were tested together in a competitive mixture. Keep them separate and do not create a pooled tetracycline capacity.

## GO feedstock, preparation and characterization
The visible Materials snippet states:
- Graphite powder was purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd. (China).
- Tetracycline hydrochloride, oxytetracycline and doxycycline hydrochloride were purchased from Genview Chemical Co.
- Water had resistivity greater than18 MΩ·cm.
- All chemicals were analytical reagent grade and used without further purification.

The GO characterization snippet states:
- GO was prepared using a modified Hummers method.
- GO was sonicated in water for30 min before AFM characterization.
- AFM indicates mostly single-layer GO with a topographic height of1.198 nm.
- TEM images show flake-like GO morphology.
- GO characterization methods include AFM, TEM, FTIR and UV–Vis spectroscopy. A Varian GBC Cintra10e UV–Vis spectrometer with a 1 cm quartz cuvette is mentioned.

The publisher preview does not disclose the complete modified-Hummers reagent quantities, acid/oxidant ratios, GO yield, wash-water volume, drying procedure, process energy or a monetized synthesis cost. The feedstock is commercial graphite, not a waste-derived material. Do not claim this paper demonstrates low-cost or waste-derived GO manufacture.

## Capacity and kinetic claims at abstract level
The publisher abstract reports:
- Langmuir theoretical maximum adsorption capacity for TC:313 mg/g.
- The authors say this theoretical Langmuir maximum is in close agreement with measured data.
- Pseudo-second-order kinetics are described as an excellent fit.
- PSO rate constant: k=0.065 g mg−1 h−1.
- TC adsorption capacity decreases as pH or Na+ concentration increases.
- The authors propose π–π interaction and cation–π bonding as key TC–GO interactions.

The number313 mg/g is explicitly described as the theoretical maximum calculated using the Langmuir model. It is therefore a model-derived qmax, not a separately tabulated observed qe. The phrase that it is close to measured data does not provide the numerical measured qe or the conditions needed to verify that comparison.

The PSO rate constant is available, but the preview does not show the fitted kinetic qe, initial concentration, adsorbent mass, solution volume, pH, temperature, contact time, R², RMSE or other fit errors. Do not infer an observed qe or a condition-specific capacity from the rate constant.

## Isotherm-model wording inconsistency
The abstract states that the adsorption isotherm fits Langmuir and Temkin models well.

The publisher Highlights instead state that the isotherms fit Langmuir and Freundlich models well.

This is a genuine source wording discrepancy. Preserve both formulations until the full article/table is checked; do not silently harmonize Temkin to Freundlich or vice versa. The preview does not expose model coefficients, fit statistics or residual/error comparisons that would resolve it.

## Solution chemistry and target-specific response
The abstract gives a qualitative statement that TC uptake decreases with increasing pH and Na+ concentration. Exact:
- pH range and controlled values;
- sodium concentration range;
- ionic strength;
- initial TC concentrations;
- GO dose and solution volume;
- contact time and temperature;
- pH/speciation table;
are not visible in the preview.

The article's proposed mechanism is π–π interaction and cation–π bonding. This is a qualitative mechanistic explanation from the authors; the accessible preview does not provide sufficient FTIR/pH or modelling details to independently quantify the contribution of either interaction.

The preview says OTC and doxycycline adsorption isotherms were discussed and compared, but it does not expose their numerical capacities or enough conditions to compare them quantitatively with TC.

## Mass-balance and quantitative comparison boundary
No initial concentration C0, solution volume V, GO mass m, equilibrium concentration Ce or directly observed qe is available for the TC capacity statement. Therefore, no feed-limited qe ceiling can be calculated and the reported313 mg/g cannot be checked against available adsorbate mass.

Do not:
- treat313 mg/g as measured qe;
- insert it into a condition-matched numeric ranking;
- infer C0, dose, volume, pH, time or temperature from unrelated GO studies;
- treat the PSO rate constant as a fitted qe or a direct uptake value;
- assign values to OTC or doxycycline when none are shown in the accessible primary text.

## Reuse, real-water and cost evidence
No adsorption–desorption regeneration procedure, cycle number, capacity-retention value, real wastewater matrix, metal leaching, cost per mass or volume treated, GO yield, energy inventory, reagent recovery, LCA or TEA is exposed in the accessible publisher preview. The article uses aqueous solutions; the available preview does not establish performance in actual wastewater or a natural water matrix.

## Semester-paper decision
Retain SEM-024 for qualitative GO–TC interaction and solution-chemistry context, plus an abstract-reported model capacity:
1. Langmuir theoretical qmax=313 mg/g, attributed to TC, explicitly model-derived and preview-level.
2. PSO kinetic rate constant k=0.065 g mg−1 h−1, without fitted qe or goodness-of-fit statistics in the accessible preview.
3. Qualitative decline in TC uptake with higher pH and Na+ concentration.
4. Proposed π–π and cation–π interactions.
5. Separate OTC/DOX isotherm discussion, with no numeric capacities exposed.
6. Abstract says Langmuir+Temkin; Highlights say Langmuir+Freundlich. Preserve the difference until full tables are obtained.

Do not treat313 mg/g as observed qe, use the source in a condition-matched ranking, infer low-cost production, or claim real-water or regeneration performance. The source's exact Better BibTeX key remains unverified in the inspected citation export; do not invent one.
