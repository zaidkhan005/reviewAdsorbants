## SEM-038 — N-rGO

The full Wiley PDF confirms, rather than resolves, the tetracycline finite-feed discrepancy. Methods §2.3 gives 80 mL antibiotic feed plus 20 mL N-rGO dispersion at 0.5 mg/L. At Figure 2a, 10 µg/L TC in the stated 80 mL feed gives 0.8 µg TC and 0.01 mg N-rGO, so the strict ceiling is 80 mg/g; Table 1 reports observed qe=90.21 mg/g. Figure 2c at 1 µg/L TC and dose 40–100 µg/L plots ≈88–90 mg/g despite a lower finite-feed ceiling (≈8–25 mg/g depending denominator). Table 1 fitted kinetic qe values are 93.13 (first order, R²=.9579) and 92.84 mg/g (second order, R²=.9291). Table 2 TC Langmuir qm=662.25 mg/g is fitted. The 1265.82 mg/g abstract maximum belongs to clindamycin, not TC. Hold TC capacities from ranking pending author clarification. Ion/humic acid/competition tests are simulated; HA 10 mg/L reduces uptake up to 30%. The ≤25% decline over eight cycles is not TC-specific capacity retention.

## SEM-047 — Amin et al. (2026)

The 28-run RSM optimum is pH 5.5, 44 min, dose 1.0 g/L, C0=10 mg/L, with 98.5% removal. Table 5 gives experimental qe=9.89 mg/g and double-exponential fitted qe=9.94 mg/g. Table 4 shows Sips is best fit (qm=746.4 mg/g, ms=.46, R²=.998); Langmuir qm=161.4 mg/g is a separate inferior fitted output. River-water removal is 89.34%, with pH 7.67 and TOC 4.9 mg/L, but initial TC concentration/spiking basis is not stated. Ten-cycle adsorption efficiency ≈99→80% and desorption ≈98→75% with 0.01 M HCl; not capacity retention. No quantified cost inventory.

## SEM-050 — Lemaoui et al. (2026)

Municipal wastewater was 0.45 µm PES-prefiltered and deliberately spiked. Main single-solute TET 5 mg/L membrane qe=17.3±0.5 mg/g; separate low-concentration 500/1000 µg/L tests gave removals 97.8/95.4% and qe=2.45/4.77 mg/g. Four-drug co-spiked MWW TET removal=85.4±1.8%, qe=16.9 mg/g. Six 0.1 M NaOH cycles retained 65.4±0.9% performance versus 30.4±1.2% for GO. Leaching bounds were checked. USD 2.5–6.8/m² and 14–59 kg CO2-eq/m² apply to the active layer only, not full-module cost or financial cost per treated volume. Powder-batch qe=22.7±0.6 mg/g remains quarantined pending exact Figure 1 C0/volume/mass basis; qm=323.6 mg/g is fitted.

## SEM-051 — Saheed et al. (2026)

Time test: C0=200 mg/L, 20 mL, 20 mg beads, pH 4, 120 min; removal 94.93%, derived qe≈189.86 mg/g. Concentration series: 50–250 mg/L, 20 mg/20 mL, pH 7, 120 min; removal 74.64→60.08%, derived qe≈37.32→150.2 mg/g. Langmuir qm=267.11/277.78 mg/g is fitted; Hill is best fit (R²=.9939 nonlinear, .9869 linear). Table 8 reports bead cost USD 0.541/g and author estimate USD 1.95/g TC, but uses linear Langmuir despite Hill being best and an illustrative 1 g/L feed above the 250 mg/L maximum tested. Desorption Methods say 0.1 M while Results/Figure 10 use 1.0 M; 83.9% is desorption efficiency, not capacity retention. No real-water/flow test.

## Additional unselected paper — Kabir et al. (2024)

“Green Reduction of Waste-Battery-Derived Graphene Oxide by Jute Leaves and Its Application for the Removal of Tetracyclines from Aqueous Media,” DOI 10.1021/acssusresmgt.4c00181. This is not SEM-010 and is not in the current candidate register, frozen selected 49 or V2 bibliography. It uses spent dry-cell graphite, modified-Hummers GO oxidation, and jute-leaf extract to reduce GO to GrGO. At TEC C0=100 ppb (0.1 mg/L), pH 6, dose 0.175 g/L, 60 min and 25 °C, 98% removal implies derived qe≈0.56 mg/g. Its Langmuir qm=22.85 mg/g has poor R²=.59 while Freundlich R²=.992. Reuse shows six cycles with removal ≈97→92%, although the conclusion mentions seven. Waste-derived precursor and plant reduction do not prove low cost; acids/oxidants, washes, heating, centrifugation and drying remain uncosted.
