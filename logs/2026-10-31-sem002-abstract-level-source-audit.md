## What the accessible primary abstract supports
The study tests graphene oxide nanosheets (GOS) for adsorption of three antibiotics in aqueous solution:
- Ciprofloxacin (CPN; CIP)
- Tetracycline (TCN; TC)
- Doxycycline (DCN)

The abstract says initial concentration, pH, temperature and contact time were studied. It describes pH about6–7 and contact time about100–200min as optimal ranges. It reports that the maximum adsorption capacity decreases as temperature increases from25 to45°C.

The abstract/highlights provide a fitted/model-derived capacity up to173.4mg/g for CPN. It explicitly gives the comparative same-temperature fitted-capacity order:
**CPN ≫ DCN > TCN.**

This is ordinal information only for the TCN endpoint; the accessible abstract does not state a numerical TC-specific qmax, observed qe or removal percentage. It is not permissible to interpolate TC's capacity from the CPN capacity or the ordinal ordering.

## Models and mechanism reported at abstract level
- Equilibrium models: Hill and Toth are reported as the best models from the authors' non-linear regressions/eight error functions.
- Kinetics: Elovich is reported for CPN; pseudo-second-order is reported for TCN and DCN.
- Mechanism: π–π interaction and cation–π bonding are proposed/interpreted by the authors.
- The abstract does not provide the complete model parameters, fitting statistics, target-specific concentrations, adsorbent doses, solution volumes or conditions linking each qmax to a test point.

The 173.4mg/g capacity must be explicitly attributed to CPN, not TCN. The capacity order does not quantify how far TCN is below DCN or CPN.

## Cost, preparation and application evidence
The accessible abstract does not provide the GOS precursor feedstock identity, synthesis recipe, material yield, acid/oxidant/solvent inventory, energy use, regeneration procedure, real-water tests, leaching or monetized cost/TEA/LCA. The phrase “pharmaceutical waste” in the article title refers to the antibiotics being treated; it is not evidence that the GOS feedstock itself is waste-derived.

Do not infer that this is a low-cost or waste-derived adsorbent from the title or abstract.

## Decision for the semester paper
Retain SEM-002 / EV-SEM-017 as qualitative, abstract-level context showing that adsorbent performance and kinetic model can differ by antibiotic target. Do not:
1. Enter a numeric TC qmax/qe/removal value.
2. Attribute the CPN 173.4mg/g result to TC.
3. Estimate a TCN value from the ordinal CPN≫DCN>TCN ranking.
4. Add a numeric condition-labelled comparison row without the primary tables/figures.
5. Claim regeneration, real-water suitability, waste-derived feedstock or low production cost from the abstract.

The next step for quantitative use would be access to the full article tables/figures; that is not currently present in the Library search and no upload has been requested. The existing Zotero export had no verified citation key for this work; do not invent one.
