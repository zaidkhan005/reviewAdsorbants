## Adsorbent variants and selection
The study evaluates three magnetic Fe3O4/rGO formulations:
- Fe3O4@SiO2/rGO
- HT-Fe3O4/rGO
- CP-Fe3O4/rGO

The publisher snippet states that CP-Fe3O4/rGO performs best for both tetracycline hydrochloride (TCH) and ofloxacin (OFL). The abstract reports an overall specific surface area of 95.12 m²/g for the Fe3O4/rGO composite system. Do not assume this area is formulation-specific for all three variants unless the full table confirms it.

The publisher preview describes co-precipitation for Fe3O4/rGO synthesis, but the detailed preparation steps, Fe3O4:rGO ratios and silica/hydrothermal/citrate details distinguishing the three variants are not visible.

## Rotating magnetic field: important field-on/field-off qualification
The publisher's “Selection of the adsorbent composite” snippet explicitly says that Fe3O4@SiO2/rGO, HT-Fe3O4/rGO and CP-Fe3O4/rGO were tested with and without RMF and that adsorption was higher in the presence of RMF for all three variants. Thus, a qualitative field-off comparator is present; it would be incorrect to state that the source lacks a field-free comparison entirely.

However, the accessible preview does not expose the numerical field-on versus field-off removal/capacity values, the percentage enhancement, or the specific formulation-level difference. Do not quote an RMF enhancement percentage from this source preview.

The only numerical field setting visible is an excitation current of 2 A. Current alone is not an energy metric. The preview does not disclose:
- RMF frequency;
- magnetic flux density or field strength;
- coil geometry or distance to the sample;
- voltage, resistance, electrical power or duty cycle;
- waveform or field uniformity;
- operating duration attributable to each adsorption run;
- energy consumption per litre treated or per gram antibiotic removed.

Consequently, do not claim that the RMF process is low-energy, energy-efficient or economically advantageous on the basis of the 2 A current alone. It is a process-intensification concept whose energy cost cannot be calculated from the accessible publisher text.

## Kinetics, isotherms and mechanisms
The abstract reports:
- Langmuir isotherm analysis;
- pseudo-second-order (PSO) kinetic behavior;
- π–π interactions and hydrogen bonding as dominant adsorption contributions.

The Conclusion snippet gives PSO rate constants in the range 0.0005–0.0009 g/(mg·min). The preview does not provide formulation-specific rate constants, fitted qe values, Langmuir qmax values, R², error functions or model tables. Do not infer qmax or qe from the >96% removal result.

The authors' mechanism interpretation should be treated as a proposed explanation supported by the full paper's characterization/analyses, not as a quantitative partitioning of interactions. The preview alone does not provide the detailed FTIR/XPS/zeta-potential evidence needed to quantify electrostatic, π–π or hydrogen-bond contributions.

## Regeneration and water-matrix evidence
The publisher abstract states that removal efficiency remains above 87% after four cycles. The accessible preview does not give:
- the regeneration/elution reagent;
- concentration and eluent volume;
- desorption time or washing/drying protocol;
- per-cycle TCH and OFL removal values;
- whether “>87% efficiency” means removal efficiency in a repeated adsorption run or retained adsorption capacity.

Therefore, report it only as an author-reported >87% efficiency after four cycles. Do not describe it as >87% capacity retention or a quantified regeneration efficiency without the full figure/method.

The abstract also says the system shows robust performance in complex water matrices. However, the preview does not expose matrix composition, exact coexisting ions/organic matter, whether the water is real or synthetic, matrix-specific removal values, or the numerical data. Phrase this as an author-reported complex-matrix robustness claim, not verified performance in actual municipal/industrial wastewater.

## Semester-paper decision
Retain SEM-033 as an emerging RMF-assisted magnetic rGO example with separate TCH and OFL optimum pH conditions. The defensible preview-level statements are:
1. TCH: >96% removal at C0=30 mg/L, dose1.0 g/L, RMF current2 A, pH5.
2. OFL: >96% removal at C0=30 mg/L, dose1.0 g/L, RMF current2 A, pH7.
3. Adsorption is higher under RMF than without RMF for all three tested composites, but no numerical enhancement ratio is exposed.
4. CP-Fe3O4/rGO is identified as the best-performing composite; the overall area is reported as95.12 m²/g.
5. PSO kinetics and Langmuir isotherm are reported; PSO k range0.0005–0.0009g/(mg·min); numerical qe/qmax and fit statistics are unavailable.
6. >87% efficiency after four cycles is author-reported; do not relabel as capacity retention.
7. A complex-water robustness claim is reported, but matrix composition and numerical outcomes are not visible.

Do not:
- combine TCH and OFL values;
- infer measured qe/qmax from removal percentages;
- infer energy use or cost from the 2 A excitation current;
- claim an exact field enhancement percentage;
- claim real-water or cost-effectiveness without the underlying data.
