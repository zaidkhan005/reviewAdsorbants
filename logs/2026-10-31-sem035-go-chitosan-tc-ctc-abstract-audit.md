## Material architecture and target separation
The abstract identifies two GO/chitosan forms:
1. Graphene-oxide/chitosan film (GOCSF).
2. Graphene-oxide/chitosan particles (GOCSP).

The two materials are prepared by solution blending and casting. The study varies precursor ratios and solution pH and evaluates tetracycline (TC) and chlortetracycline (CTC) adsorption separately. Do not combine TC and CTC capacities into one pooled antibiotic value.

The abstract reports that GOCSP has higher structural integrity, larger specific surface area and greater cross-linking density than GOCSF. SEM-EDS, BET, XPS and FT-IR are used for morphological and physicochemical characterization.

## Abstract-reported adsorption findings
- Both GOCSF and GOCSP have an optimum adsorption pH of5.
- GOCSP maximum capacities: TC3.69mg/g; CTC2.51mg/g.
- GOCSF maximum capacities: TC3.50mg/g; CTC2.25mg/g.
- The publisher attributes the adsorption behavior to the pseudo-second-order kinetic model and the Langmuir isotherm.
- The process is described as endothermic.
- The abstract describes the materials as environmentally benign and easy to regenerate, but gives no regeneration method or cycle values.

The capacities are reported in the abstract as “maximum adsorption capacities,” alongside the statement that Langmuir is the preferred isotherm model. It is reasonable to describe them as abstract-attributed Langmuir-type maxima, but the unavailable model table prevents checking exact fitted qmax parameters, fit statistics or whether the authors' phrase maps unambiguously to qmax. They are not directly reported measured qe values in the accessible abstract.

## What remains unavailable
The official publisher landing page and abstract do not provide:
- GO:chitosan precursor ratios or the detailed formulation for GOCSF versus GOCSP;
- exact blending, casting, particle formation, drying or curing conditions;
- initial concentrations, solution volume, adsorbent mass/dose, temperature or contact time;
- measured equilibrium qe or percentage-removal data;
- numerical Langmuir/PSO fit parameters or R²/RMSE values;
- exact TC and CTC concentration ranges, or matrix-specific operating conditions beyond pH5;
- desorption/eluent composition, regeneration cycle count or retained-capacity data;
- real wastewater/natural-water validation;
- material yield, reagent/energy inventory, normalized treatment cost, LCA or TEA.

The publisher lists SEM-EDS/BET/XPS/FTIR methods and qualitative structural differences, but no full results tables are accessible. “Easy regeneration” must remain a qualitative author claim, not a demonstrated cycle result.

## Semester-paper decision
Retain SEM-035 as a relevant GO–chitosan film/particle study for separate TC and CTC targets. The defensible abstract-only entries are:
- GOCSP: TC3.69mg/g; CTC2.51mg/g.
- GOCSF: TC3.50mg/g; CTC2.25mg/g.
- Optimum pH5; PSO kinetics and Langmuir isotherm; endothermic adsorption.

Do not:
1. Call these capacities directly observed qe.
2. Add a condition-labelled numeric row or compare them against matched-condition capacities without dose/C0/volume/time/temperature.
3. Treat “easy regeneration” as verified multi-cycle reuse.
4. Claim real-water validation or measured low-cost performance.
5. Infer GO:chitosan ratios or synthesis conditions from related studies.
