## Main findings supported by the abstract
- The study compares graphene with granular activated carbon (GAC) as a conventional benchmark for CIP adsorption.
- Graphene reaches equilibrium within approximately 3 minutes and adsorbs CIP faster than GAC.
- The publisher/PubMed explicitly describe the graphene maximum capacity of 323 mg/g as a **Langmuir-fitted q_max**, not an observed qe.
- Pseudo-second-order kinetics is reported as the better kinetic fit.
- Graphene adsorption decreases as pH increases, consistent with pH-dependent CIP speciation.
- Ionic strength was varied over 0.01–0.5 mol/L; natural organic matter (NOM) was tested at 5 mg/L; the study also included river-water or drinking-water source tests.
- The abstract states that these matrix influences are less adverse for graphene than for GAC. Exact retention/removal percentages and effect sizes are not exposed in the accessible abstract.
- The authors state that effective separation/recovery of graphene from water is required for practical application.

## Quantitative interpretation and limitations
The reported 323 mg/g is a model-fitted Langmuir maximum capacity. It is not a directly measured equilibrium uptake value and should not be placed in an observed qe ranking. The accessible sources do not expose:
- initial CIP concentration or concentration range;
- adsorbent dose and solution volume;
- exact pH range/optimum and temperature;
- isotherm fitting concentration range or Langmuir coefficients;
- PSO fitted qe, rate constant and goodness-of-fit values;
- exact ionic-strength/NOM/water-source effect sizes;
- GAC capacity and comparative kinetic parameters;
- separation/recovery efficiency, regeneration cycles or retained capacity;
- preparation cost, energy, normalized cost per gram/volume treated, TEA or LCA.

The paper is useful for qualitative context on rapid adsorption and graphene's relative tolerance to salts, NOM and water-source complexity compared with GAC, but it does not support condition-matched quantitative comparison in the current evidence set.

## Scope boundary
Graphene is the relevant material for the semester comparison. GAC is retained only as the conventional benchmark in this study; it is not counted as a core graphene-family material. The abstract's river/drinking-water tests indicate some source-water assessment, but the exact matrix compositions and performance values are unavailable, so do not claim a fully quantified real-water validation.

## Semester-paper decision
Retain SEM-020 / EV-SEM-014 as a contextual graphene-versus-GAC CIP study. In manuscript synthesis:
1. Quote Langmuir-fitted q_max=323 mg/g only with that exact model label.
2. Report approximately 3-minute equilibration as an abstract-level statement.
3. Note that graphene was less adversely affected than GAC by ionic strength (0.01–0.5 mol/L), NOM (5 mg/L) and the tested river/drinking-water sources, without inventing effect percentages.
4. Highlight the need for effective graphene separation/recovery.
5. Exclude 323 mg/g from condition-matched numeric rankings until the full Methods/isotherm data are available.
