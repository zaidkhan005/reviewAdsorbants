## Editing principles

- Preserve every study-specific numerical result, operating condition, unit, endpoint distinction, equation, citation key, and source-access qualification.
- Do not convert fitted model outputs into observed uptake or merge removal, capacity-retention, and desorption-efficiency measures.
- Do not weaken or remove mass-balance holds, source-internal discrepancies, matrix limitations, or cost-denominator caveats.
- Improve readability primarily by shortening long sentences, splitting run-on constructions, clarifying subjects and antecedents, and reducing redundant wording.
- Leave source tables, evidence-ledger rows, citation keys, bibliography records, and figure/diagram code unchanged.

## Main improvements

1. **Abstract and Introduction:** sharpened the distinction between adsorption and degradation; improved the framing of solution chemistry, material design, and low-cost potential; removed awkward or repetitive constructions.
2. **Methods:** clarified the focused-search description, source-access tiers, eligibility wording, and endpoint/cost assessment language.
3. **Results and discussion:** improved the presentation of endpoint distinctions, mass-balance caveats, source-access limitations, multicomponent and flow-through results, and preparation-cost examples. Long composite-study paragraphs were divided into shorter statements without changing the reported measurements.
4. **Mechanistic interpretation:** clarified that spectral changes, pH responses, DFT/MD outputs, and kinetic/isotherm fits provide different kinds of support and do not prove one universal mechanism.
5. **Practical feasibility:** improved readability across preparation, recovery, regeneration, matrix validation, and cost discussions; preserved distinctions among real, simulated, and spiked water and among different cost units.
6. **Conclusions:** strengthened the progression from demonstrated adsorption to the limits of affordability claims and stated future reporting needs in parallel form.
7. **Terminology and spelling:** standardized common variants to American English (for example, behavior, modeled, modeling, labeled, and favor).
