## Summary of source review

### 1) Xiang et al. (2022) — lignin-impregnated biochar

- The pristine corn-stalk and wheat-straw chars were produced at 600 °C for 5 h. Lignin treatment involved 1 g lignin/100 mL water, 24 h contact at 25 °C, washing/drying and another 600 °C/5 h calcination.
- Table 1 (PDF p. 3) reports surface area from 211.55 to 478.39 m²/g across CS, WS, CS-L and WS-L; TPV increases to 0.3087 cm³/g for WS-L.
- Figure 2 (PDF p. 4) reports capacities 13.30 mg/g (CS), 16.63 mg/g (WS), 21.48 mg/g (CS-L) and 31.48 mg/g (WS-L).
- The authors report highest uptake at pH 7; Na+, K+, Mg2+ and Al3+ reduce uptake, while Ca2+ enhances it (PDF p. 6, Fig. 5 and adjacent text).
- **Critical caveat:** supplied methods describe laboratory solutions prepared in deionized water, not a sampled wastewater matrix. Treat as synthetic-water adsorption evidence, not validation in actual wastewater. The supplementary tables and parameter data are referenced but not present in the uploaded PDF.

### 2) Zhao et al. (2023) — alkaline-modified rice-straw biochar

- The starting rice-straw biochar was produced at 450 °C; KRBC was prepared by treating 10 g RBC with 200 mL of 2 M KOH for 4 h, washing and drying at 60 °C.
- Table 2 (PDF p. 3) reports BET area 109.382→130.520 m²/g and pore volume 0.031→0.128 cm³/g (4.13×), with average pore diameter 4.227→6.747 nm.
- Batch methods use purchased SMT/OTC/AMX solutions prepared for experiments (25 mL of 30 mg/L solution, 0.08 g char, 25 ± 1 °C, 180 rpm, dark, up to 2880 min; duplicate). Isotherms use the stated ranges through 50 mg/L for SMT/AMX and 200 mg/L for OTC.
- Table 3 (PDF p. 5) gives Langmuir fitted Qm (mg/g) for RBC/KRBC: SMT 4.52/6.47; OTC 87.8/51.7; AMX 2.01/7.67. The same table has generally higher R²/lower RMSE for Freundlich than Langmuir, especially poor Langmuir fits for OTC; do not treat its fitted Qm values as reliable, directly comparable experimental capacity rankings.
- In the three-antibiotic mixture at 30 mg/L each and 3.2 g/L KRBC over 48 h, authors report capacities OTC 2.924, SMT 2.222 and AMX 0.686 mg/g (PDF p. 7).
- **Critical caveat:** while the title and abstract say livestock wastewater, the supplied main text does not describe collection or pretreatment of a livestock-wastewater sample. Experimental procedures describe purchased antibiotic standards and prepared aqueous solutions; only antibiotic/metal cocktails are described for multi-contaminant tests. Unless the missing SI confirms real wastewater, use this as controlled lab solution evidence, not real-wastewater validation.

### 3) Mohammadzadeh et al. (2025) — magnetite-pine bark and pilot columns

- MPB is a magnetite-modified pine-bark biosorbent, not a biochar. Pilot columns used a mixed bed: BC 2.25 kg + MPB 0.5 kg for the lower bed, then BC 0.78 kg in the upper bed.
- Batch comparison used 3 g/L sorbent, 450 mL actual municipal MBR effluent, 200 rpm and 3 h (PDF p. 6, Table 2). Antibiotic removal for MPB/BC/AC was: azithromycin 75.6/11.5/87.2%; sulfadiazine 28.5/16.6/76.2%; sulfamethoxazole 64.8/31.5/81.5%; trimethoprim 82.2/12.2/98.5%.
- The pilot column had a 21 L empty-bed volume, ran for almost four months at about 0.90 L/h and ~24 h contact time. Table 3 reports removal for the BC+MPB bed of azithromycin 92.3%, clarithromycin 71.4%, clindamycin 86.7%, metronidazole 45.9%, sulfadiazine 38.6%, sulfamethoxazole 58.3%, trimethoprim 99.7% (PDF pp. 8–10). The paper's 76.5% overall average covers all pharmaceutical analytes, not antibiotics alone.
- Regeneration used 0.2 M NaOH (46 L for 48 h in the pilot column), followed by 25 L tap water for 24 h. Wash effluent iron reached 1500 μg/L after 24 h and 2500 μg/L after 48 h. The paper reports a short-term toxicity pulse and increased effluent bacterial counts following regeneration (PDF pp. 4, 10–11).
- Author cost estimate for MPB was 1.43–1.53 €/kg compared with purchased AC at about 3.30 €/kg (PDF p. 11, §3.8). This is an adsorbent-production price estimate, not a full techno-economic analysis or life-cycle assessment; authors themselves call for fuller sustainability assessment.
- **Critical caveats:** real MBR effluent had already undergone biological treatment and target pharmaceutical concentrations were very low; the pilot figures reflect the mixed BC+MPB bed, not MPB alone; regeneration adds caustic/rinse-water and iron-leaching concerns; supporting information is referenced but not included in the upload.
