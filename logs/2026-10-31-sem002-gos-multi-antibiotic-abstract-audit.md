## Target separation
The paper evaluates three separate antibiotics on graphene oxide nanosheets (GOS) in aqueous solution:
- Ciprofloxacin (CPN; CIP)
- Doxycycline (DCN)
- Tetracycline (TCN; TC)

Do not pool the three target drugs or assign the CPN result to TC.

## Publisher-preview claims that are supported
The abstract states:
- Factors studied: initial concentration, pH, temperature and contact time.
- Approximate optimum initial pH: 6–7 for each drug.
- Approximate optimum contact time: 100–200 min for each drug.
- The monolayer adsorption capacity decreases as temperature increases from 25 to 45 °C.
- Eight error functions were used in nonlinear-regression/model selection.
- Hill and Toth isotherm models represented the equilibrium adsorption data.
- Pseudo-second-order kinetics were reported for TCN and DCN, while Elovich kinetics were reported for CPN.
- Fitted maximum capacity ranking at a common temperature: CPN ≫ DCN > TCN.
- CPN has a reported maximum capacity up to 173.4 mg/g.
- The publisher highlights and abstract attribute the mechanism interpretation to π–π interactions and cation–π bonding.

The 173.4 mg/g headline belongs to **ciprofloxacin (CPN)**. It is not a tetracycline value. The abstract does not expose a numerical q_max, q_e, removal percentage or condition-specific capacity for TCN.

## Isotherm-model nuance
The abstract describes Hill and Toth as the equilibrium models representing the adsorption data, but separately says the cross-drug maximum-capacity order was obtained from the Sips equation. Preserve that distinction:
- Hill/Toth are identified as the preferred equilibrium descriptions in the abstract.
- The reported CPN ≫ DCN > TCN q_max order is specifically attributed to Sips at a common temperature.

Do not collapse these into the assertion that all reported q_max values are Hill/Toth parameters, and do not interpolate a numerical TC value from the ordinal order.

## Preparation and practical evidence boundary
The available publisher section snippet says stock antibiotic solutions were freshly prepared in distilled water and pH was adjusted using acid/base. It does not expose a complete GO synthesis recipe, adsorbent mass/dose, solution volume, concentration series, exact drug-specific condition vectors, fit coefficients, or numeric TCN results.

The title's phrase “pharmaceutical waste” refers to the adsorbate/pollutant context; it is not evidence that the GO adsorbent was synthesized from waste feedstock. The study should be described as GO nanosheets used to adsorb antibiotic pharmaceuticals, not as waste-derived GO.

No verified real-water or wastewater-matrix trial, regeneration cycle protocol, cycle-resolved retention, normalized cost, product yield, energy inventory, LCA or TEA is visible in the accessible abstract/preview.

## Semester-paper decision
Retain as qualitative, multi-antibiotic GO context only:
- It supports that the target drug affects fitted adsorption performance and kinetic model selection.
- The authors report the fitted capacity order CPN ≫ DCN > TCN under a same-temperature comparison.
- The study reports PSO kinetics for TCN/DCN and Elovich kinetics for CPN.
- It reports broad optimum pH/contact-time ranges, not a condition-complete TCN performance point.

Do not:
1. Attribute 173.4 mg/g to TC; it is the CPN maximum.
2. Insert a numeric TCN q_max or observed q_e from the ordinal ranking.
3. Treat the title phrase “pharmaceutical waste” as a waste-derived adsorbent claim.
4. Include a condition-labelled numeric TCN comparison row without the primary table/full text.
5. Claim real-water validation, regeneration or cost evidence from the abstract.
