## Individual source pins

### CLM-73 — Hossain et al. (2026), DOI 10.1039/D6RA00494F

- **Key:** `hossainRemovalCiprofloxacinAqueous2026`; evidence EV-SEM-026.
- **Source:** official open-access RSC/PMC full text.
- **Pins:** synthesis §§2.2–2.4; practicality/cost §3.10, Tables 2–3 and Figs. 18–19; §3.11, Table 4/Figs. 20–21; §3.13.
- **Source value:** bead price is reported as USD 10–30/kg. The cost scenario uses 0.1 g/L, while a key experimental dose is 10 g/L (100× higher).
- **Derived screen:** 10–30 USD/kg beads and observed q_e=7.15 mg/g yield an initial bead-purchase ratio of ~USD 1,399–4,196/kg CIP captured. Using fitted Langmuir q_max=10.14 mg/g gives ~USD 986–2,959/kg CIP. These are calculations from purchase price and loading only; they are not reported lifecycle costs and exclude replacement, reuse loss, regeneration, preparation yield, energy, washing, disposal and treatment equipment.
- **Disposition:** reject the source's low-cost advantage until an auditable cost worksheet resolves the dose basis and full boundary.

### CLM-74 — Lemaoui et al. (2026), DOI 10.1007/s42114-026-01808-5

- **Key:** `lemaouiSurfaceAssembledGraphene2026`; evidence EV-SEM-046.
- **Source:** user-supplied primary Springer paper and supplementary information; the cost/lifecycle section is also indexed on the official Springer source.
- **Source estimates:** active-layer material cost USD 2.5–6.8/m², based on GO (assumed USD 20–100/kg), levulinic acid (~USD 2/kg), TOPO (~USD 20/kg), composition ~46.8 wt.% GO/53.2 wt.% DES and ~124 g/m² areal loading. Active-layer cradle-to-gate footprint 14–59 kg CO₂-eq/m².
- **Normalization assumption:** the paper converts this embodied footprint to about 0.02–0.08 kg CO₂-eq/m³ under a two-year service-life scenario and approximately 800 m³/m² treated at 50 LMH; NaOH regeneration adds about 1–3 g CO₂-eq/m³.
- **Disposition:** do not represent USD/m² as financial cost per m³, or kg CO₂-eq/m³ as monetary cost. Price-year/currency basis, module/support replacement, full energy and transport/end-of-life boundaries are not established. The municipal wastewater was 0.45 µm prefiltered and deliberately spiked; the membrane result is not native unspiked wastewater performance.

### CLM-75 — Saheed et al. (2026), DOI 10.1080/16583655.2026.2613597

- **Key:** `saheedCitricAcidCrosslinked2026`; evidence EV-SEM-047.
- **Source:** user-supplied primary Taylor & Francis PDF (25 pages); Table 8, economic discussion, isotherm hierarchy and batch concentration range inspected 3 Nov 2026.
- **Source estimates:** Table 8 reports bead cost USD 0.541/g and an author estimate of USD 1.95/g TC removed using laboratory chemical/energy inputs and a Malaysian electricity tariff.
- **Qualification:** calculation uses linear Langmuir q_max=277.78 mg/g although Hill is the best-fitting model; it illustrates 1 g/L TC even though tested C₀ reaches only 250 mg/L. Itemized prices, price year, yield and the complete calculation worksheet were not recovered.
- **Disposition:** retain as an author-reported scenario, not independently reproduced full-system TEA or proof of scale-up affordability.

## Cost-comparison rules retained

1. USD/kg of adsorbent is not USD/kg of antibiotic captured, and neither is lifecycle operating cost.
2. USD/m² active-layer material is not financial USD/m³ treated.
3. kg CO₂-eq/m² or kg CO₂-eq/m³ is an environmental footprint, not monetary cost.
4. Author USD/g removed values depend on model choice, feed scenario, sorbent dose, regeneration assumptions, price year and tariff; those assumptions must be exposed.
5. No reviewed study establishes a comparable, independently reproducible full-system financial cost per m³ treated and per mass of antibiotic removed.
