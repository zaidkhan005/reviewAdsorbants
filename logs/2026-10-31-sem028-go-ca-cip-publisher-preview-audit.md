## What the primary preview establishes
- Adsorbent: graphene oxide/calcium alginate (GO/CA) biocomposite fibers.
- Preparation route: wet spinning.
- Target: ciprofloxacin (CPX/CIP).
- GO loading: up to 6 wt%.
- Optimum pH: 5.9.
- Equilibrium contact time: 24 h.
- Isotherm description: Freundlich model fits the adsorption data well.
- Kinetic description: pseudo-second-order (PSO) model fits the kinetic data well.
- The source presents two separate numerical endpoints, described below.

## Two author-reported endpoints — do not conflate
### Endpoint A: removal percentage
The publisher highlights and abstract report:
- Removal percentage: 78.9%.
- Adsorbent dose: 2 g/L.
- GO loading: 6 wt%.

The preview does not expose the initial CPX concentration, solution volume, temperature, exact pH used for this specific point, or a table reference that would fully define the batch. The general optimum pH5.9 and 24 h equilibrium time are reported elsewhere in the abstract/conclusion, but the preview does not explicitly state that every value belongs to this exact 78.9% run. Therefore, do not attach missing conditions to Endpoint A as if directly reported.

### Endpoint B: equilibrium adsorption capacity
The abstract reports that the adsorption capacity increases from 18.45 mg/g to 39.06 mg/g as GO loading in the fibers increases from 0 wt% to 6 wt%, at an equilibrium ciprofloxacin concentration of 60 mg/L.

For the GO-containing fibers:
- GO loading: 6 wt%.
- Equilibrium CPX concentration (Ce): 60 mg/L.
- Reported adsorption capacity: 39.06 mg/g.

This is described as adsorption capacity at an equilibrium concentration. The preview does not call 39.06 mg/g a Langmuir qmax; the preferred isotherm is Freundlich. Do not re-label 39.06 mg/g as fitted qmax. The initial concentration, liquid volume, bead/fiber mass, temperature and paired batch setup remain unavailable in the preview.

The accessible primary source does not say whether Endpoint A (78.9% removal at 2 g/L and 6 wt% GO) and Endpoint B (39.06 mg/g at Ce=60 mg/L and 6 wt% GO) are measurements from the same experimental run. Keep them separate. Do not derive a q_e from 78.9% removal, infer the initial concentration from Ce, or use the two values as a matched removal-capacity pair.

## pH, kinetics and isotherm model statements
- The optimum pH for CPX adsorption onto GO/CA fibers is 5.9.
- The equilibrium contact time is 24 h.
- Freundlich is reported as the better-fitting isotherm model.
- Pseudo-second-order is reported as the better-fitting kinetic model.
- The publisher preview contains no Freundlich coefficients, PSO qe/k2, R², RMSE, chi-square or other model parameters. No numerical fitted qmax is exposed.

Do not report 39.06 mg/g as a qmax or infer a Langmuir fit. Do not invent PSO parameters or exact equilibrium-time data beyond the stated 24 h.

The complete GO oxidation recipe, GO and alginate concentrations, fiber-forming dope ratios, spinneret/needle details, coagulation-bath concentration, fiber residence time, washing, drying, yield and GO distribution are not visible in the primary preview. Do not reproduce a full wet-spinning recipe from analogous GO/alginate papers.

## Mass balance and quantitative limitations
No exact initial CPX concentration, batch volume and residual concentration are accessible for the 78.9% removal point. For the 39.06 mg/g value, Ce=60 mg/L is reported but C0, volume and fiber mass are not. Thus:
- no feed-mass ceiling can be independently calculated;
- no observed qe can be reconstructed from the removal endpoint;
- no direct comparison can be made between 78.9% removal and 39.06 mg/g capacity;
- no exact condition-matched ranking against other CIP adsorbents is justified.

Keep the value 39.06 mg/g as a reported equilibrium adsorption capacity at Ce=60 mg/L and 6 wt% GO, not as qmax.

## Reuse, cost and real-water evidence
No regeneration/desorption, adsorption–desorption cycles, retained capacity, regenerant, or cycle-resolved results are exposed in the accessible publisher preview. No normalized material cost, process energy, product yield, washing-water inventory, solvent recovery, cost per mass treated or lifecycle assessment is shown. No actual municipal, industrial or natural-water test is reported in the accessible source. The introduction discusses wastewater treatment as motivation, but that is not an actual wastewater trial.

## Semester-paper decision
Retain SEM-028 as preview-level evidence for GO/calcium-alginate wet-spun fibers and the effect of GO loading on CIP adsorption. Report separately:
1. 78.9% removal at 2 g/L adsorbent dose and 6 wt% GO;
2. equilibrium adsorption capacity 39.06 mg/g at Ce=60 mg/L and 6 wt% GO (from 18.45 mg/g at 0 wt% GO);
3. general optimum pH5.9 and equilibrium time24h;
4. Freundlich isotherm and PSO kinetic model preferences.

Do not:
- assume both endpoints arise from the same batch;
- assign a missing C0, volume, temperature or initial pH to either endpoint;
- label 39.06 mg/g as fitted qmax;
- infer q_e from 78.9% without the initial concentration, liquid volume and fiber mass;
- claim regeneration, real wastewater performance or quantified low-cost production.
