# Project charter

## Working topic

Adsorptive removal of antibiotics from water and wastewater using materials spanning raw biomass, natural clays and biopolymers, biochar, and engineered/nano-carbon adsorbents.

## Core research question

How do adsorbent processing intensity and material architecture relate to adsorption performance, regeneration potential, energy/cost burden, and practical deployment for antibiotic removal?

## Intended contribution

Build a comparative processing–cost–performance framework. Avoid an uncritical “low cost versus high capacity” narrative. Compare studies only with explicit attention to target contaminant, feedstock/material, treatment/activation route, initial concentration, pH/speciation, adsorbent dose, contact time, water matrix, and performance metric.

## Questions to address

1. What processing steps are needed to prepare each material class?
2. Which studies report observed uptake, and which report model-fitted maximum capacities?
3. How do pH, antibiotic speciation, competing solutes, and concentration change performance?
4. What evidence supports each proposed adsorption mechanism?
5. What is known about desorption, regeneration, real wastewater, column operation, and spent-adsorbent management?
6. Are energy, chemical use, capacity-normalized cost, life-cycle impacts, or scale-up reported—or absent?

## Evidence quality principles

- Prefer original research for numerical claims and methods.
- Record contradictions or impossible-looking values; do not silently “correct” them.
- Do not compare removal percentages across different doses, concentrations, contact times, or matrices as if they were directly comparable.
- Distinguish adsorption from degradation/oxidation and membrane separation.
- Treat claims of “low cost”, “sustainable”, and “green” as hypotheses until supported by cost, process, or life-cycle evidence.
- Verify journal quartiles by source, year, and subject category before Q1-only inclusion.
