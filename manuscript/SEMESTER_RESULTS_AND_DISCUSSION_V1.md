## 3.1. Reported adsorption performance is condition-dependent, not a universal capacity leaderboard

The evidence assembled so far does not support a single ranking of graphene-based adsorbents by one reported “capacity.” The available studies report different endpoints—observed equilibrium uptake (`q_e`), kinetic-model-fitted `q_e`, isotherm-fitted `q_{max}` or `q_m`, percentage removal, and reuse-cycle removal—under different initial concentrations, doses, pH values, contact times, temperatures and matrix compositions. These quantities answer different questions and cannot be placed in one numeric order without further harmonization. The condition-labelled working table therefore records these endpoints in separate fields rather than ranking them ([comparison table](../data/semester-condition-labelled-comparison.csv)).

For tetracycline (TC), the graphene oxide/calcium-alginate fiber study reports observed `q_e=17.6` mg/g at an initial concentration of 10 mg/L and `q_e=65.4` mg/g at 60 mg/L; both values came from separate 25°C, 5-hour equilibrium tests at a dose of 0.50 g/L. Its Langmuir-fitted `q_{max}=131.6` mg/g is a separate model parameter, not the measured uptake under either feed condition. The study also reports a highest removal of 56.8% at pH 6 in a separate pH test using 50 mg/L TC; that percentage should not be paired with the two `q_e` values as though all three results came from the same experiment. \cite{sem004Zhu2018GOAlginateFiber} [evidence: EV-SEM-002]

Another TC example shows why the endpoint and temperature must remain attached to the number. Carboxymethylcellulose-functionalized magnetic graphene oxide reportedly removed 96.04% of TC at an initial concentration of 2 mg/L, a dose of 0.1 g/L, pH 5, 25°C and 25 minutes. A mass-balance calculation from those conditions gives approximately 19.21 mg/g, but this is a derived check rather than a directly quoted experimental `q_e`. Its linear Langmuir `q_{max}=54.73` mg/g was fitted at 50°C. These are not contradictory by themselves: they are different metric types at different temperatures. The derived value and fitted parameter should be reported separately, not treated as interchangeable evidence of capacity. \cite{sem018Dipannita2025CMCFunctionalizedMGO} [evidence: EV-SEM-007]

A magnetic graphene-oxide/fibrillated-cellulose composite has a reported observed TC uptake of `q_e=93.36\pm3.85` mg/g at an initial concentration of 70 mg/L and dose of 0.50 g/L; its fitted Sips parameter is `q_m=130.11` mg/g. The source extraction associates that dose test with pH 5 and a 960-minute contact period, but the temperature for this specific `q_e` result has not yet been pinned down. The result is useful as a condition-specific composite measurement, but it should not be used in a condition-matched ranking until that missing condition is checked. \cite{sem017Dang2025MagneticGOFibrillatedCellulose} [evidence: EV-SEM-006]

For ciprofloxacin (CIP), a zinc–carbon-battery-derived graphene oxide study reports 99.0% removal at an initial concentration of 2 mg/L, dose of 20 mg/L, pH 5.7, 30°C and 20 minutes. The same paper reports a Langmuir-fitted `q_{max}=419.62` mg/g at 30°C and, in a separate kinetic test, an observed `q_e=78.82` mg/g with a pseudo-second-order fitted `q_e=79.36` mg/g. The removal result and the fitted isotherm capacity cannot be collapsed into a single performance score; the accessible extraction does not establish that the kinetic-test concentration and dose match the low-concentration optimum. The reported result is a useful low-concentration removal case, not proof of low total treatment cost. \cite{sem010Yasmin2024ZnCGrapheneOxide} [evidence: EV-SEM-004]

## 3.2. High fitted capacities require both a condition label and a source-quality check

The full-text PGOT@GEL study reports a Langmuir-fitted `q_{max}=821.24` mg/g for CIP (isotherm discussion, printed p. 8). The separate response-surface validation reports measured `q_e=807.54` mg/g versus a predicted 800.60 mg/g at `C_0=296.52` mg/L, pH 6.70, PGOT@GEL dose 5.08 mg and 298.88 K (RSM discussion, printed p. 9, §3.3.6). At that `q_e` and adsorbent mass, about 4.10 mg of CIP must have been adsorbed; the initial solution volume would therefore need to be at least about 13.84 mL at the stated `C_0` for the mass balance to be feasible. The main paper's §2.6 (printed p. 3) refers to the Supporting Materials for the full adsorption-experiment details, but the solution volume is not reported in the main PDF and the supporting file was not present in the project files located for this pass. This means the validation value cannot yet be independently mass-balanced; it does not establish that the value is wrong. A separate pH test reports `q_e=192.24` mg/g and 96.12% removal at pH 7. These are distinct endpoints. The source also has a matrix-label inconsistency: the prose refers to real-water samples while Figure 12 captions the application test as simulated real wastewater (printed pp. 10–11). The high fitted value is evidence of strong reported uptake under the studied conditions, not by itself proof of trace-level treatment, verified real-wastewater performance or low cost. Cite the study with the verified key `\cite{liThreedimensionalGrapheneOxidebased2025}`.

The sulfonated graphene oxide (SGO) CIP study reports a fitted Langmuir `q_{max}=1000` μmol/g (331.35 mg/g) and a separate experimental `q_e=237.12` μmol/g (approximately 78.6 mg/g) at an initial concentration of 50 μmol/L, pH 4, 25°C and dose 0.2 g/L. Table 1 reports the latter experimental uptake, while the text reports 96.53% removal at the nominally same 25°C condition. Using the paper’s stated dose and mass-balance equation gives approximately 94.85% removal from `q_e=237.12` μmol/g, so that `q_e`/removal pair retains a real consistency flag. The source pin matters here: the separate kinetic/concentration series covers 10–100 μmol/L (Table 1, printed p. 40822), whereas the Langmuir isotherm series in Figure 10 spans 50–1000 μmol/L at 0.2 g/L and pH 4 (printed p. 40824); Table 2 reports the fitted `q_m=1000` μmol/g across 25–40°C (printed p. 40825). Therefore, a 500 μmol/g ceiling calculated from the separate 100 μmol/L kinetic series must not be applied to this isotherm fit. Keep the fitted parameter distinct from measured `q_e`, preserve the removal discrepancy, and do not silently alter either source value. Cite the study with the verified key `\cite{shahaExploringAdsorptionEfficiency2025}`.

The nitrogen-doped reduced graphene oxide (N-rGO) paper is relevant to TC and trace-antibiotic treatment, but its numeric capacities require a source-basis caveat. The methods state that 80 mL of antibiotic solution was combined with 20 mL of N-rGO dispersion at 0.5 mg/L (printed p. 1300, §2.3), while Table 1 reports experimental `q_e` values of 80.58–90.21 mg/g for the nominal `C_0=10` μg/L kinetic tests (printed p. 1302, Figure 2 and Table 1). The method does not clarify whether `C_0` denotes the concentration in the original 80 mL solution or the final 100 mL mixture. On the first interpretation, the initial antibiotic mass and 0.01 mg of N-rGO imply an upper uptake of about 80 mg/g; if 10 μg/L instead denotes the final 100 mL system, the ceiling is about 100 mg/g. Thus the upper Table 1 values expose an unresolved concentration/volume basis, not a settled arithmetic correction. Table 2 reports fitted Langmuir `q_m` values including 662.25 mg/g for TC and 1265.82 mg/g for clindamycin (printed p. 1303), far above the directly observed kinetic `q_e` values; retain these as model outputs only and exclude them from cross-study capacity ranking pending unit, dosage and fit-range reconciliation. Existing source-linked evidence remains in CORP-031 / EV-B10-004–EV-B10-009; do not re-extract it as a duplicate. Cite with the verified key `\cite{zhangAdsorptionCharacteristicsNrGO2022}`.

Other headline values remain less secure. For example, the graphene-versus-granular-activated-carbon CIP article reports a Langmuir-fitted capacity of 323 mg/g and equilibrium within approximately three minutes in its publisher abstract, but the full Methods/tables/figures were not available in the current Library view. This supports a provisional source-level claim about rapid adsorption and matrix sensitivity; it is not yet a condition-validated capacity comparison. GAC is a benchmark in that study, not a core material family in this review. Cite with `\cite{zhuCiprofloxacinAdsorptionGraphene2015a}`, but keep the capacity explicitly marked as abstract-level pending full-table verification.

## 3.3. Graphene-containing composites add practical benefits, but also processing and regeneration burdens

Composite and magnetic formats can improve separation or handling, yet those advantages should be evaluated together with the full recovery procedure. The cellulose-nanofibril/GO aerogel study reports TC removal of 82.1% in an across-antibiotic comparison, a fitted Langmuir `q_m\approx454.5`–454.6 mg/g and a pseudo-second-order model-fitted `q_e=84.8` mg/g. Its ten-cycle result reports TC removal of 78.9% after reuse compared with 82.1% in the initial comparison. Regeneration uses 5 wt.% NaOH for five hours, followed by washing and vacuum freeze-drying. The fitted isotherm capacity and kinetic-model `q_e` are not observed endpoints, and the exact condition for the first across-antibiotic comparison is not fully pinned in the figure caption. The material is useful for discussing immobilization and reuse, but its cost claim cannot be treated as demonstrated economics without a cost/energy inventory. Cite with `\cite{yao3DAssemblyBased2017}`.

The graphene study conducted at 200 μg/L per antibiotic provides a complementary low-concentration, mixed-solute context. Seven antibiotics were tested together at pH 7, 298 K and 120 rpm with 1 g/L graphene; TC approached adsorption equilibrium in about 2.5 hours. The text reports a 89.3–100% removal range across the seven antibiotics, but the exact TC-specific percentage is figure-based and has not been extracted. It must not be guessed from the across-drug range. The experiment is a mixed synthetic solution, not a single-solute TC isotherm or direct validation in untreated wastewater. The paper’s biochar comparator data are outside the core graphene comparison. Cite with `\cite{pengAdsorptionAntibioticsGraphene2016}`.

Two additional configurations illustrate why high removal does not automatically translate into low-cost superiority. A GO/Co–Fe magnetic composite reports TC removal of 94.1% in one part of the article and 93.94% in another at an initial concentration of 0.2 mg/L, dose 0.15 g/L, pH 7, 25°C and 30 seconds. Its fitted Langmuir `q_{max}=64.10` mg/g was measured at 50°C, a separate temperature. A rotating-field Fe₃O₄/rGO configuration reports over 96% tetracycline-hydrochloride removal at an initial concentration of 30 mg/L and dose 1.0 g/L, pH 5 and an excitation current of 2 A. The latter abstract does not state a numerical `q_{max}` or observed `q_e`, and 2 A is not a measurement of electrical energy use. Neither configuration can be called cheaper or superior without the missing conditions, matched controls, power/energy and recovery data. \cite{sem034Hossain2024GOCoFeTetracyclines,sem033Ding2026RotatingFieldFe3O4rGO} [evidence: EV-SEM-027 and EV-SEM-037]

## 3.4. Multicomponent, matrix and flow-through results must remain distinct

Multicomponent results are valuable because they test competition, but their fitted parameters should not be mixed with single-solute capacities. For sulfonated graphene oxide (SGO), the full text reports Langmuir-fitted \(q_m\) values at 25 °C of 370.37 mg/g for the binary ciprofloxacin–moxifloxacin system, 357.14 mg/g for the ternary ciprofloxacin–moxifloxacin–levofloxacin system, and 333.33 mg/g for the quaternary system (Table 3, printed p. 10). These are mixture-system fitted outputs, not isolated-ciprofloxacin \(q_{max}\) values. The stated mixed-solution isotherm range is 18.24–371.64 mg/L, with SGO at 0.2 g/L and pH 5 (Methods §2.4, printed p. 3; Fig. 6, p. 9); the results therefore describe high-concentration synthetic mixtures, not trace-level conditions. Importantly, the abstract claims suitability for treating real wastewater, but the authors' own limitations section says real-wastewater samples were not tested (§3.12, printed p. 14). The study should not be cited as demonstrated real-water validation. It also reports over 97% antibiotic desorption with DMF containing 1 mol/L HCl and over 92% initial performance retained after five adsorption–desorption cycles; the solvent/acid recovery, waste handling and process cost are not quantified (Fig. 12, printed p. 13; §3.12, p. 14). \cite{sem031Shaha2026SulfonatedGrapheneOxide} [evidence: EV-SEM-036]

Flow-through evidence should also be reported separately from batch adsorption. The GO–alginate permeable-reactive-barrier study is potentially important for CIP-contaminated groundwater because the publisher abstract reports a barrier application, approximately 72-hour equilibrium and a reported maximum adsorption capacity of 100 mg/g at pH 7. However, the abstract also says the data follow a Freundlich model, so the meaning of the stated “maximum capacity” cannot be determined from the abstract alone. The lifetime calculation, cost values and column conditions are also unavailable in the current extraction. It should be treated as an application lead rather than assumed to be a Langmuir maximum or a verified cost estimate. \cite{sem037Zhao2018AlginateGOBarrier} [evidence: EV-SEM-011]

## 3.5. “Low-cost potential” remains a hypothesis until costs are normalized

The evidence reviewed here is substantially stronger on laboratory adsorption results than on comparative economics. Most studies do not report a full-process cost inventory containing adsorbent yield, reagent quantities and prices, energy, washing, drying, regeneration, solvent recovery, residual handling and a denominator such as cost per volume treated or mass of CIP/TC removed. The preparation and recovery burden can also be substantial: strong-acid/oxidant GO synthesis, NMP use, freeze-drying, long alkaline regeneration, methanol or DMF/HCl washing, high adsorbent doses and ultrasound or rotating-field energy are all process inputs that need to be counted rather than omitted.

One GO/PVA/calcium-alginate bead paper reports a cost estimate, but its cost calculation assumes 0.1 g/L while an experimental 0.5 g per 50 mL condition corresponds to 10 g/L—a 100-fold difference in dose. The method also alternates between 50 mL and 55 mL wording. Its cost conclusion is therefore not independently reproducible from the available extraction. \cite{sem029Hossain2026GOPVACA} [evidence: EV-SEM-026]

Accordingly, the term *low-cost potential* should be assessed as a multi-criteria question, not inferred from a large fitted `q_{max}`, a waste-derived precursor, easy magnetic separation, or a high percentage removal by itself. At present, the evidence supports condition-specific adsorption findings and identifies process-cost gaps; it does not yet support a universal claim that one graphene-based material is the lowest-cost option.

## 3.6. Limits that must be preserved in the final manuscript

1. **Metric type:** keep measured `q_e`, fitted `q_{max}/q_m`, kinetic-model-fitted `q_e`, removal percentage and column metrics separate.
2. **Condition comparability:** do not compare capacities measured at different concentrations, doses, pH values, temperatures, matrix compositions or models as a simple ranking.
3. **Source integrity:** preserve the source-specific inconsistencies for studies actually discussed here: the SGO `q_e`/removal mass-balance mismatch; the unresolved concentration/volume basis and unusually large fitted values in the N-rGO study; the GO/Co–Fe removal-percentage discrepancy; and the GO/PVA/calcium-alginate dose/cost and method-volume inconsistencies. The 500 μmol/g ceiling previously calculated from the separate 10–100 μmol/L SGO kinetic series is not valid for the Langmuir fit, because Figure 10 reports an isotherm range of 50–1000 μmol/L. Caveats for other screened candidates (including GO–LDH, EDTA-functionalized GO and chitin/nGO column data) remain in the evidence register; do not imply they were evaluated in this draft unless they are added and cited.
4. **Citation readiness:** all 15 in-text citation keys in this working draft resolve to entries in `references/semester-manuscript-cited-references.bib`. Six keys were verified against the provided Better BibTeX export; the other nine are project-local keys and have not been confirmed as the keys generated by live Zotero/Better BibTeX. Reconcile those records in Zotero and re-export before final submission; this is a synchronization task, not a remaining placeholder in the draft.
5. **Cost evidence:** an author's “low-cost” or “economical” statement remains an author claim unless the cost boundary, inputs, date/currency and denominator are reported.
