## 3.1. Reported adsorption performance is condition-dependent, not a universal capacity leaderboard

The evidence assembled so far does not support a single ranking of graphene-based adsorbents by one reported “capacity.” The available studies report different endpoints—observed equilibrium uptake (`q_e`), kinetic-model-fitted `q_e`, isotherm-fitted `q_{max}` or `q_m`, percentage removal, and reuse-cycle removal—under different initial concentrations, doses, pH values, contact times, temperatures and matrix compositions. These quantities answer different questions and cannot be placed in one numeric order without further harmonization. The condition-labelled working table therefore records these endpoints in separate fields rather than ranking them ([comparison table](../data/semester-condition-labelled-comparison.csv)).

For tetracycline (TC), the graphene oxide/calcium-alginate fiber study reports observed `q_e=17.6` mg/g at an initial concentration of 10 mg/L and `q_e=65.4` mg/g at 60 mg/L; both values came from separate 25°C, 5-hour equilibrium tests at a dose of 0.50 g/L. Its Langmuir-fitted `q_{max}=131.6` mg/g is a separate model parameter, not the measured uptake under either feed condition. The study also reports a highest removal of 56.8% at pH 6 in a separate pH test using 50 mg/L TC; that percentage should not be paired with the two `q_e` values as though all three results came from the same experiment. \cite{zhuAdsorptionTetracyclineAntibiotics2018} [evidence: EV-SEM-002]

Another TC example shows why the endpoint and temperature must remain attached to the number. Carboxymethylcellulose-functionalized magnetic graphene oxide reportedly removed 96.04% of TC at an initial concentration of 2 mg/L, a dose of 0.1 g/L, pH 5, 25°C and 25 minutes. A mass-balance calculation from those conditions gives approximately 19.21 mg/g, but this is a derived check rather than a directly quoted experimental `q_e`. Its linear Langmuir `q_{max}=54.73` mg/g was fitted at 50°C. These are not contradictory by themselves: they are different metric types at different temperatures. The derived value and fitted parameter should be reported separately, not treated as interchangeable evidence of capacity. \cite{dipannitaCarboxymethylcelluloseFunctionalizedMagnetic2025} [evidence: EV-SEM-007]

A magnetic graphene-oxide/fibrillated-cellulose composite has a reported observed TC uptake of `q_e=93.36\pm3.85` mg/g at an initial concentration of 70 mg/L and dose of 0.50 g/L; its fitted Sips parameter is `q_m=130.11` mg/g. The source extraction associates that dose test with pH 5 and a 960-minute contact period, but the temperature for this specific `q_e` result has not yet been pinned down. The result is useful as a condition-specific composite measurement, but it should not be used in a condition-matched ranking until that missing condition is checked. \cite{dangIntegrationMagneticGraphene2025} [evidence: EV-SEM-006]

For ciprofloxacin (CIP), a zinc–carbon-battery-derived graphene oxide study reports 99.0% removal at an initial concentration of 2 mg/L, dose of 20 mg/L, pH 5.7, 30°C and 20 minutes. The same paper reports a Langmuir-fitted `q_{max}=419.62` mg/g at 30°C and, in a separate kinetic test, an observed `q_e=78.82` mg/g with a pseudo-second-order fitted `q_e=79.36` mg/g. The removal result and the fitted isotherm capacity cannot be collapsed into a single performance score; the accessible extraction does not establish that the kinetic-test concentration and dose match the low-concentration optimum. The reported result is a useful low-concentration removal case, not proof of low total treatment cost. \cite{yasminEfficientRemovalCiprofloxacin2024} [evidence: EV-SEM-004]

## 3.2. High fitted capacities require both a condition label and a source-quality check

The full-text PGOT@GEL study reports a Langmuir-fitted `q_{max}=821.24` mg/g for CIP (isotherm discussion, printed p. 8). The separate response-surface validation reports measured `q_e=807.54` mg/g versus a predicted 800.60 mg/g at `C_0=296.52` mg/L, pH 6.70, PGOT@GEL dose 5.08 mg and 298.88 K (RSM discussion, printed p. 9, §3.3.6). At that `q_e` and adsorbent mass, about 4.10 mg of CIP must have been adsorbed; the initial solution volume would therefore need to be at least about 13.84 mL at the stated `C_0` for the mass balance to be feasible. The main paper's §2.6 (printed p. 3) refers to the Supporting Materials for the full adsorption-experiment details, but the solution volume is not reported in the main PDF and the supporting file was not present in the project files located for this pass. This means the validation value cannot yet be independently mass-balanced; it does not establish that the value is wrong. A separate pH test reports `q_e=192.24` mg/g and 96.12% removal at pH 7. These are distinct endpoints. The source also has a matrix-label inconsistency: the prose refers to real-water samples while Figure 12 captions the application test as simulated real wastewater (printed pp. 10–11). The high fitted value is evidence of strong reported uptake under the studied conditions, not by itself proof of trace-level treatment, verified real-wastewater performance or low cost. Cite the study with the verified key `\cite{liThreedimensionalGrapheneOxidebased2025}`.

The sulfonated graphene oxide (SGO) CIP study reports a fitted Langmuir `q_{max}=1000` μmol/g (331.35 mg/g) and a separate experimental `q_e=237.12` μmol/g (approximately 78.6 mg/g) at an initial concentration of 50 μmol/L, pH 4, 25°C and dose 0.2 g/L. Table 1 reports the latter experimental uptake, while the text reports 96.53% removal at the nominally same 25°C condition. Using the paper’s stated dose and mass-balance equation gives approximately 94.85% removal from `q_e=237.12` μmol/g, so that `q_e`/removal pair retains a real consistency flag. The source pin matters here: the separate kinetic/concentration series covers 10–100 μmol/L (Table 1, printed p. 40822), whereas the Langmuir isotherm series in Figure 10 spans 50–1000 μmol/L at 0.2 g/L and pH 4 (printed p. 40824); Table 2 reports the fitted `q_m=1000` μmol/g across 25–40°C (printed p. 40825). Therefore, a 500 μmol/g ceiling calculated from the separate 100 μmol/L kinetic series must not be applied to this isotherm fit. Keep the fitted parameter distinct from measured `q_e`, preserve the removal discrepancy, and do not silently alter either source value. Cite the study with the verified key `\cite{shahaExploringAdsorptionEfficiency2025}`.

The nitrogen-doped reduced graphene oxide (N-rGO) paper is relevant to TC and trace-antibiotic treatment, but its numeric capacities require a source-basis caveat. The methods state that 80 mL of antibiotic solution was combined with 20 mL of N-rGO dispersion at 0.5 mg/L (printed p. 1300, §2.3), while Table 1 reports experimental `q_e` values of 80.58–90.21 mg/g for the nominal `C_0=10` μg/L kinetic tests (printed p. 1302, Figure 2 and Table 1). The method does not clarify whether `C_0` denotes the concentration in the original 80 mL solution or the final 100 mL mixture. On the first interpretation, the initial antibiotic mass and 0.01 mg of N-rGO imply an upper uptake of about 80 mg/g; if 10 μg/L instead denotes the final 100 mL system, the ceiling is about 100 mg/g. Thus the upper Table 1 values expose an unresolved concentration/volume basis, not a settled arithmetic correction. Table 2 reports fitted Langmuir `q_m` values including 662.25 mg/g for TC and 1265.82 mg/g for clindamycin (printed p. 1303), far above the directly observed kinetic `q_e` values; retain these as model outputs only and exclude them from cross-study capacity ranking pending unit, dosage and fit-range reconciliation. Existing source-linked evidence remains in CORP-031 / EV-B10-004–EV-B10-009; do not re-extract it as a duplicate. Cite with the verified key `\cite{zhangAdsorptionCharacteristicsNrGO2022}`.

The graphene-versus-granular-activated-carbon (GAC) ciprofloxacin study is a useful conventional benchmark, but its numbers remain abstract-level in this audit. The publisher and PubMed abstracts report that graphene reached equilibrium within approximately 3 min and had a Langmuir-fitted \(q_{max}=323\) mg/g. They also report that the adverse effects of ionic strength (0.01–0.5 mol/L), natural organic matter (5 mg/L), and the tested water sources (river water or drinking water) were less significant for graphene than for GAC. The abstract does not reveal the initial concentration, adsorbent dose, temperature, numeric pH range, isotherm concentration range, water-matrix preparation or exact effect sizes; the complete article and supplemental file were not retrievable in this audit. Accordingly, the study supports a qualitative claim about rapid adsorption and relative matrix sensitivity, but the fitted 323 mg/g value remains excluded from condition-matched numerical ranking. The authors also condition practical application on proper graphene separation and recovery; the accessible abstract does not provide a monetized recovery cost or verified regeneration-cycle data. GAC is an industrial benchmark in this comparison, not a core material family in this review. \cite{zhuCiprofloxacinAdsorptionGraphene2015a}

## 3.3. Graphene-containing composites add practical benefits, but also processing and regeneration burdens

Composite and magnetic formats can improve separation or handling, but those advantages must be weighed against the recovery procedure. The full-text cellulose-nanofibril/GO (CNF/GO) aerogel article reports TC removal of 82.1% in its 21-antibiotic screen (Fig. 7), compared with 78.9% TC removal after ten adsorption–desorption cycles (Fig. 13). The general batch method specifies 8 mg CNF/GO in 40 mL, 160 rpm, 25 °C overnight, with initial concentrations in the 1–100 mg/L range and pH adjusted from 2 to 12; however, Fig. 7's caption does not identify one specific initial concentration, pH or dose, so the 82.1% point remains unsuitable for a condition-matched removal ranking. Separate TC factor tests must not be assumed to be the Fig. 7 conditions. Table 1 gives a pseudo-second-order model-fitted \(q_e=84.8\) mg/g (R²=0.99), while Table 2/Fig. 9 gives Langmuir-fitted theoretical \(q_m=454.6\) mg/g (R²=0.99); the conclusion rounds the same \(q_m\) to 454.5 mg/g. These model-derived values are not observed uptake and remain separate from removal percentage. Regeneration uses 5 wt.% NaOH for 5 h, followed by washing and vacuum freeze-drying. No monetized cost or energy inventory is reported, so the article's economic language is not a verified cost assessment. Cite with \cite{yao3DAssemblyBased2017}.

The seven-antibiotic graphene study provides a low-concentration mixed-solute context. Its experimental design uses 100 mg sorbent in 100 mL containing each antibiotic at 200 ng/mL (200 μg/L), pH 7, 298 K and 120 rpm, equivalent to 1 g/L graphene. The Results text states that TC adsorption by graphene approached equilibrium in about 2.5 hours. It reports an 89.3–100% removal range across all seven drugs for graphene, not a TC-specific percentage; that exact point is plotted in Fig. 2 but is not stated numerically in the text. I therefore do not infer the TC percentage from the full range. The reported mean equilibrium uptake for graphene, 190.3 μg/g, is averaged across the seven antibiotics and is not TC-specific. This experiment is a synthetic mixed solution, not a single-solute TC isotherm or direct validation in untreated wastewater; the paper's biochar comparator results remain outside the core graphene comparison. Cite with \cite{pengAdsorptionAntibioticsGraphene2016}.

Two additional configurations illustrate why high removal does not automatically translate into low-cost superiority. The GO/Co–Fe magnetic composite paper reports tetracycline (TC) removal of 94.1% in its abstract and §3.2.2 (Fig. 5b), 93.91% in §3.2.4 (Fig. 5d), and 93.94% in §4 Conclusion. The reported optimum conditions are an initial TC concentration of 0.2 mg/L, GO/Co–Fe dose 0.15 g/L, pH 7, room temperature (about 25°C), 250 rpm and 30 s. These three source-reported percentages are an unresolved internal inconsistency and should not be averaged or silently corrected. Table 2 reports Langmuir-fitted TC \(q_m=60.24\), 62.50 and 64.10 mg/g at 30, 40 and 50°C, respectively; therefore, the 64.10 mg/g value is a fitted capacity at 50°C, not the 25°C removal experiment. Section 3.2.7/Fig. 9 reports up to eight reuse rounds after magnetic separation, methanol treatment, water washing and drying at 50°C, with removal efficiency falling from 95% to 90%. The paper does not supply a normalized cost inventory or demonstrate real-water testing. The rotating-field Fe₃O₄/rGO study reports removal above 96% for both tetracycline hydrochloride (TCH) and ofloxacin (OFL) under separate optimum conditions: an initial concentration of 30 mg/L, adsorbent dose of 1.0 g/L and excitation current of 2 A, with pH 5 for TCH and pH 7 for OFL. The composite's reported specific surface area is 95.12 m²/g, and the abstract reports above 87% efficiency after four cycles. The publisher's indexed preview indicates that adsorption was compared with and without the rotating magnetic field (RMF); all three tested Fe₃O₄/rGO formulations performed better under RMF, with CP-Fe₃O₄/rGO performing best. The authors describe Langmuir and pseudo-second-order fits, and attribute the field enhancement to particle rotation and improved external mass transfer, with π–π interactions and hydrogen bonding contributing to adsorption. However, a complete full text and its supplementary information were not accessible in this audit. The exact complex-water matrices, numerical field/no-field effect size, fitted capacity values, field frequency/strength, voltage/power, and energy per treated volume therefore remain unverified. An excitation current of 2 A is not an energy measurement, so this example does not establish energy efficiency or low-cost superiority. \cite{hossainUltrafastSimultaneousRemoval2024,dingRotatingMagneticFieldenhanced2026} [evidence: EV-SEM-027 and EV-SEM-037]

## 3.4. Multicomponent, matrix and flow-through results must remain distinct

Multicomponent results are valuable because they test competition, but their fitted parameters should not be mixed with single-solute capacities. For sulfonated graphene oxide (SGO), the full text reports Langmuir-fitted \(q_m\) values at 25 °C of 370.37 mg/g for the binary ciprofloxacin–moxifloxacin system, 357.14 mg/g for the ternary ciprofloxacin–moxifloxacin–levofloxacin system, and 333.33 mg/g for the quaternary system (Table 3, printed p. 10). These are mixture-system fitted outputs, not isolated-ciprofloxacin \(q_{max}\) values. The stated mixed-solution isotherm range is 18.24–371.64 mg/L, with SGO at 0.2 g/L and pH 5 (Methods §2.4, printed p. 3; Fig. 6, p. 9); the results therefore describe high-concentration synthetic mixtures, not trace-level conditions. Importantly, the abstract claims suitability for treating real wastewater, but the authors' own limitations section says real-wastewater samples were not tested (§3.12, printed p. 14). The study should not be cited as demonstrated real-water validation. It also reports over 97% antibiotic desorption with DMF containing 1 mol/L HCl and over 92% initial performance retained after five adsorption–desorption cycles; the solvent/acid recovery, waste handling and process cost are not quantified (Fig. 12, printed p. 13; §3.12, p. 14). \cite{shahaKineticIsothermThermodynamic2026} [evidence: EV-SEM-036]

Flow-through evidence should also be reported separately from batch adsorption. The sodium alginate/graphene-oxide hydrogel-bead study evaluates a permeable reactive barrier (PRB) for ciprofloxacin-contaminated groundwater and reports approximately 72 h to equilibrium. The authors state that flow-through experiments varied flow rate, influent ciprofloxacin concentration and ionic strength, and that a two-site chemical non-equilibrium model was used for PRB design under local hydrogeological conditions. The reported maximum adsorption capacity is 100 mg/g at pH 7; however, the abstract says the isotherm data fit Freundlich, whereas the indexed publisher conclusion explicitly calls 100 mg/g a Langmuir-model maximum. This model-selection inconsistency must remain visible rather than be silently reconciled. The publisher abstract also states that PRB lifetime and cost were calculated, but the numerical lifetime/cost outputs, assumptions, column conditions and underlying tables could not be verified from the accessible excerpts. Thus, this paper supports an application-level discussion of a groundwater PRB, but it does not yet support a quantitative cost or verified column-performance comparison. \cite{zhaoSodiumAlginateGraphene2018} [evidence: EV-SEM-011]

## 3.5. “Low-cost potential” remains a hypothesis until costs are normalized

The evidence reviewed here is substantially stronger on laboratory adsorption results than on comparative economics. Most studies do not report a full-process cost inventory containing adsorbent yield, reagent quantities and prices, energy, washing, drying, regeneration, solvent recovery, residual handling and a denominator such as cost per volume treated or mass of CIP/TC removed. The preparation and recovery burden can also be substantial: strong-acid/oxidant GO synthesis, NMP use, freeze-drying, long alkaline regeneration, methanol or DMF/HCl washing, high adsorbent doses and ultrasound or rotating-field energy are all process inputs that need to be counted rather than omitted.

One GO/PVA/calcium-alginate bead paper reports a cost estimate, but its cost calculation assumes 0.1 g/L while an experimental 0.5 g per 50 mL condition corresponds to 10 g/L—a 100-fold difference in dose. The method also alternates between 50 mL and 55 mL wording. Its cost conclusion is therefore not independently reproducible from the available extraction. \cite{hossainRemovalCiprofloxacinAqueous2026} [evidence: EV-SEM-026]

Accordingly, the term *low-cost potential* should be assessed as a multi-criteria question, not inferred from a large fitted `q_{max}`, a waste-derived precursor, easy magnetic separation, or a high percentage removal by itself. At present, the evidence supports condition-specific adsorption findings and identifies process-cost gaps; it does not yet support a universal claim that one graphene-based material is the lowest-cost option.

## 3.6. Limits that must be preserved in the final manuscript

1. **Metric type:** keep measured `q_e`, fitted `q_{max}/q_m`, kinetic-model-fitted `q_e`, removal percentage and column metrics separate.
2. **Condition comparability:** do not compare capacities measured at different concentrations, doses, pH values, temperatures, matrix compositions or models as a simple ranking.
3. **Source integrity:** preserve the source-specific inconsistencies for studies actually discussed here: the SGO `q_e`/removal mass-balance mismatch; the unresolved concentration/volume basis and unusually large fitted values in the N-rGO study; the GO/Co–Fe removal-percentage discrepancy; and the GO/PVA/calcium-alginate dose/cost and method-volume inconsistencies. The 500 μmol/g ceiling previously calculated from the separate 10–100 μmol/L SGO kinetic series is not valid for the Langmuir fit, because Figure 10 reports an isotherm range of 50–1000 μmol/L. Caveats for other screened candidates (including GO–LDH, EDTA-functionalized GO and chitin/nGO column data) remain in the evidence register; do not imply they were evaluated in this draft unless they are added and cited.
4. **Citation readiness:** all 15 in-text citation keys in this working draft resolve to entries in `references/semester-manuscript-cited-references.bib`. Direct comparison against the Library file `full ++.bib` (164 unique entries) verified six entries by exact DOI/key match; the other nine referenced items were absent by DOI/title from that export. A nine-entry import batch is now prepared at `references/zotero-import-batch-missing-nine-2026-10-29.bib`. Before importing, search Zotero by DOI to avoid duplicates; then re-export Better BibTeX and reconcile the generated keys before final submission. The current local draft has no missing-key placeholders, but synchronization is still pending.
5. **Cost evidence:** an author's “low-cost” or “economical” statement remains an author claim unless the cost boundary, inputs, date/currency and denominator are reported.
