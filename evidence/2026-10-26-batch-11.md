## 1. Kurczewska & Cegłowski (2025)
**Alginate vs chitosan composites with halloysite/molecularly imprinted polymer hybrids for tetracycline**. *International Journal of Biological Macromolecules* 307, 142000. DOI 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2025.142000. Candidate CORP-013.

**Preparation/materials.** Sodium alginate (1 g in 50 mL water, 40 °C, 400 rpm, 2 h) was mixed with 0.5 g halloysite or polymer additives for 12–24 h, dripped into 200 mL of 2% CaCl2, ionically crosslinked 24 h, washed and dried at 25 °C. Materials: ALG; ALG_Hal; ALG_MIP; ALG_NIP; ALG_Hal@MIP; ALG_Hal@NIP. The tetracycline-imprinted polymer uses MAA, EGDMA, AIBN and a TC template; halloysite was silane-functionalized before polymer deposition. BET area rises from 7.08 m²/g (ALG) to 42.04 m²/g (ALG_Hal@MIP). The article's Fig. 1 (PDF p. 4) shows bead morphology; Figs. 2–3 (pp. 5–6) show XRD/FTIR and thermal behaviour.

**Capacity and conditions.** Main condition sets use 20 mg adsorbent, 10 mL solution, 298 K; pH studies used 25 mg/L TC, pH 3–9, 48 h. Concentration series 5–700 mg/L; time study 0–72 h at 500 mg/L. ALG pHPZC = 6.15; additive composites pHPZC 6.72–6.95. Highest performance was generally at pH 5 (ALG/ALG_Hal) or pH 6 (other composites). Abstract reports maximum observed capacity 281 mg/g for ALG_Hal@MIP. Table 1 (PDF p. 8) gives fitted Langmuir q_m in mg/g: ALG 83.68; ALG_Hal 216.5; ALG_MIP 552.5; ALG_NIP 145.8; ALG_Hal@MIP 339.0; ALG_Hal@NIP 174.8. The paper generally prefers Freundlich for composites and Langmuir for pure ALG, so do not conflate the abstract/observed capacity with fitted q_m.

Time to equilibrium: ALG ~8 h; ALG_Hal ~18 h; ALG_Hal@MIP/ALG_Hal@NIP ~24 h; free-polymer systems ~48 h. Table 2 (PDF p. 9) gives q_exp 277.6 mg/g for ALG_Hal@MIP. **Mass-balance issue:** the stated 500 mg/L, 10 mL, 20 mg setup contains 5 mg TC, so the maximum theoretical uptake is 250 mg/g. The reported 277.6 mg/g exceeds that ceiling. Leave it flagged pending source clarification.

**Mechanism/regeneration/competition.** Proposed roles include pore filling, electrostatic interactions, Ca-associated complexation, H-bonding and π–π interactions. PSO fits best according to the authors, but this alone does not prove a unique mechanism; intraparticle plots indicate multiple transport stages. Regeneration used ethanol with 1 h ultrasound, then water washing; after five cycles pure ALG fell to ~63% of initial efficiency while complex alginate beads remained above 80%. Competitors included cefaclor, doxorubicin and vancomycin; competition was strongest for unmodified ALG and ALG_Hal, less severe with MIP. Doxorubicin is an anticancer pharmaceutical, not an antibiotic; retain the competitor identity precisely.

**Real water/soil.** Tap, Swarzędzkie Lake and Cybina River water were filtered and spiked to 500 mg/L TC, adjusted to pH 6. Table 4 (PDF p. 10) gives ALG_Hal@MIP q_e 253.8 mg/g (tap), 219.6 (lake), 212.5 (river). These are high-concentration spiked matrix tests, not trace-level native wastewater. Soil studies used 1 g soil + 20 mg beads in 50 mL of 0.2 or 0.5 mg/L TC in 0.005 M CaCl2, 48 h; halloysite-containing beads reduced TC desorption from soil. No TEA/LCA, column/pilot test or regeneration-solvent cost was supplied.

## 2. Guesmi et al. (2025)
**Magnetic Se-MOF embedded in chitosan/alginate for ciprofloxacin**. *International Journal of Biological Macromolecules* 314, 144274. DOI 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2025.144274. Candidate CORP-007.

**Preparation/materials.** Fe3O4 was precipitated from Fe(III)/Fe(II) salts at 2:1 under N2 at 80 °C with ammonia to pH ~10, then separated magnetically and washed. Se-MOF synthesis used 2-aminoterephthalic acid (0.56 g) and SeCl4 (0.442 g) in methanol, processed at 120 °C; 0.50 g Fe3O4 was added, stirred 2 h, then centrifuged, ethanol-washed and dried at 80 °C/12 h. For MSCA beads, 1 g chitosan in 50 mL 5% acetic acid was mixed with 2 g alginate in 50 mL water, pH adjusted to 5, 2 g MSe-MOF added, then dripped into 2% CaCl2. Beads were crosslinked in 25% epichlorohydrin for 48 h and dried at 40 °C. BET area is 420 m²/g, pore volume reported ~5.1 cm³/g, mean pore size 8.88 nm; pHPZC 5.64; magnetization 42.4 emu/g. Fig. 2 (PDF pp. 5–6) shows characterization.

**Adsorption conditions/results.** Isotherms: 0.02 g MSCA in 25 mL CIP, C0 15–550 mg/L, pH 8, 200 rpm, 25 °C. Kinetics: 0.02 g in 25 mL of 300 mg/L CIP at pH 8, 250 rpm, 25 °C, 0–100 min. Langmuir q_m = 442.4 mg/g; reported experimental q_m = 439.2 mg/g; K_L = 0.031 L/mg; R_L = 0.12. Capacity around 380 mg/g at pH 8 in the pH series; uptake approaches ~420 mg/g after 80–100 min. Removal reported >95% below 100 mg/L C0 and ~62% at 600 mg/L. RSM optimum is 0.02 g, 100 min, pH 8, predicted q_e = 440.84 mg/g; model R² 0.9918, adjusted R² 0.9812, predicted R² 0.8682. **Source-unit flag:** BBD table on PDF p. 4 labels its response “Yield (mmol/g)” although values ~24–437 are treated as adsorption capacities in mg/g elsewhere. Keep the anomaly explicit rather than silently accepting the mmol/g label.

**Other findings.** Authors propose electrostatic attraction, coordination/complexation, hydrogen bonding, π–π interaction and pore filling, based on FTIR/XPS changes. NaCl interference: removal declines from ~98% at 0 g/L to ~52% at 50 g/L (Fig. 8, PDF p. 16). Filtered wastewater spiked at 10, 50 and 100 mg/L CIP produced reported “recovery” rates of 94.6%, 88.5%, 77.8%; retain the article's term “recovery” because the wording does not clearly establish that it means removal efficiency. This is spiked wastewater, not native trace CIP. Regeneration used ethanol:HCl (7:3 v/v) for 1 h at 25 °C, then water wash/drying at 65 °C; five-cycle retention was 96.4%, 94.2%, 91.8%, 88.6%, 82.5%. No Se/Fe leaching or toxicity test, unit cost, full LCA, column pilot or spent-material fate assessment. Reagents include SeCl4, methanol and epichlorohydrin, so a “green” claim remains unverified without a process assessment.

## 3. Nguyen et al. (2023)
**Phosphoric-acid-activated sunflower seed-husk biochar**. *Bioresource Technology* 371, 128593. DOI 10.1016/j.biortech.2023.128593. Candidate CORP-020.

**Preparation.** Sunflower husk washed, dried 105 °C/24 h, ground/sieved <500 μm. SF-600 comparison biochar: 5 g feedstock pyrolysed at 600 °C for 2 h under N2 (10 °C/min, 100 mL/min), washed and dried 65 °C/24 h. Activated PSF: impregnate husk with 85% H3PO4 at a 1:3 feedstock:acid mass ratio, oven-dry 105 °C/12 h, pyrolyse as above, rinse repeatedly with DI water and dry. Specific area increases 6.2→378.8 m²/g and pore volume 0.012→0.206 cm³/g; pHPZC for SF-600 is 3.6, while PSF is negatively charged across pH 3–11. Fig. 1 (PDF p. 3) contrasts textural/thermal/surface properties.

**Targets and capacity.** The paper tests TC, CIP, sulfamethoxazole (SMX) and ibuprofen (IBP). IBP is an NSAID, not an antibiotic; count only TC/CIP/SMX in antibiotic-only synthesis. Batch tests used 40 mL, PSF dose reported as 25 mg/L, 10 mM NaNO3 background, 298 K; initial concentrations 1–20 mg/L for TC/CIP/SMX and 5–25 mg/L for IBP. Equilibrium was reported around 6 h. Table 2 (PDF p. 6) gives Langmuir fitted q_m values for SF-600→PSF (mg/g): TC 228.3→429.3; CIP 80.8→361.6; SMX 129.1→251.3; IBP 135.8→251.1. These are fitted q_m, not working capacities demonstrated in real wastewater. Kinetic PSO q_e values were TC 415.2, CIP 321.7, SMX 207.9, IBP 206.7 mg/g; PSO R² is 0.741, 0.917, 0.976, 0.990, respectively. Elovich fits are relatively strong for TC/CIP; the authors discuss multiple steps including chemisorption, external diffusion and intraparticle diffusion.

**Water chemistry and mixtures.** In pH tests (Fig. 3, PDF p. 7), TC capacity was highest around pH 7, reported 437.6 mg/g; pH effects varied by compound. Anion tests used 10 mM Cl−, NO3−, HCO3−, CO3²−, SO4²−; Fig. 4 (PDF p. 9) reports nitrate promotion and stronger inhibition by divalent carbonate/sulfate than monovalent anions. Competitive experiments used equal 0.025 mM concentrations; uptake order IBP < SMX < CIP < TC (Fig. 5, PDF p. 9). No real wastewater, regeneration cycle, column/pilot run, product yield, unit cost or LCA was reported. H3PO4 is used at a 3:1 acid:feedstock mass ratio with repeated washing; cost and environmental superiority are therefore not quantitatively established.

## Cross-paper synthesis
- Biopolymer/clay/MIP composites (ALG_Hal@MIP) improve TC selectivity and preserve >80% efficiency after five cycles, but spiked-water concentration is 500 mg/L and an q_exp mass-balance issue needs checking.
- The Se-MOF/chitosan-alginate composite reports ~442 mg/g fitted CIP capacity and magnetic recovery, but saline performance drops substantially, its BBD response unit is inconsistent, and selenium leaching/process impacts remain unassessed.
- H3PO4 activation improves sunflower biochar area and fitted capacities substantially vs SF-600, but uses a high acid:feedstock ratio, has no reuse study and lacks TEA/LCA.
- All three lack a complete harmonized process-cost analysis. Keep observed removal, qe and fitted q_m separate. The user need not provide SI; missing details remain flagged.
