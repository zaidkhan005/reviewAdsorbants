## A. Gopal & Mukherjee (2024) — Fe/Cu CNS-embedded alginate–CMC–chitosan beads for norfloxacin
*RSC Advances* 14 (2024), 15862–15875. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1039/d4ra01971g. Existing candidate **DISC-006**. Primary experimental study; engineered biopolymer/mineral–metal nanocomposite.

### Material and preparation
Clay nanosheets (CNS) were ultrasonicated/exfoliated and loaded with Fe/Cu nanoparticles by in-situ reduction. Alginate (5 wt%) and carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC, 2 wt%) formed an aqueous mixture; the nanocomposite was introduced, droplets gelled in 2 wt% CaCl2, and beads were coated by soaking in 1 wt% chitosan prepared in glacial acetic acid. Beads were washed and vacuum-dried overnight; average diameter was about 2 mm. This is a multi-step, engineered composite—not a raw biomass or unmodified biopolymer benchmark.

### Adsorption results and model outputs
The abstract/conclusion report about **86% NOR removal** around initial concentration 20 mg/L, pH 6 and 300 min. The paper reports a Langmuir fitted **qmax = 354.9 ± 41 mg/g** (R² = 0.92; RL = 0.238), with Freundlich R² = 0.82 and Temkin R² = 0.92. This fitted maximum is not the observed equilibrium uptake at the single optimized removal test.

Table 1 lists kinetic-model estimates of qe(cal): pseudo-first-order 107.339 mg/g (R² = 0.986) and pseudo-second-order 200 mg/g (R² = 0.935); the authors identify the PFO fit as better. These fitted qe values should not be presented as observed capacities. The stated sorbent dose/concentration varies across the abstract, methods and figure captions (including 2000 mg/L, 0.15 g, 0.15 g/L and 0.30 g in 200 mL), so exact dose conditions must be verified before cross-paper comparisons.

### Solution chemistry, matrices and reported degradation
The paper reports that increasing NaCl reduced NOR removal from 86.07% to 16.59%; at the highest CaCl2 concentration, removal was 13.44%. Humic acid and polyethylene microplastics also interfered with removal. The natural water tests used tap, lake and groundwater spiked with **20 mg/L NOR**; reported removal was only about **24–28%**. The paper also discusses a simulated hospital effluent, not untreated native hospital wastewater.

At 300 min, the authors report 86.06% NOR removal versus 72.84% TOC removal, and use LC–MS to discuss demethylation, defluorination, piperazine-ring opening and oxidation. This implies the composite system contributes to catalytic transformation as well as adsorption. The paper reports 77% *Scenedesmus* viability for treated NOR solution versus 36% with untreated NOR solution. Beads were eluted in 0.1 M HCl for 12 h and the authors state use over five adsorption/desorption cycles with minimal decline.

### Important caveats
The methods give UV–vis LOD = 2.72 mg/L and LOQ = 8.24 mg/L. At 20 mg/L initial concentration, 86% removal implies an approximate residual of 2.8 mg/L, below that LOQ. The kinetic table's PSO qe(cal) of 200 mg/g also exceeds the simple mass-balance ceiling if 0.15 g/L is the dose at 20 mg/L (ceiling ≈133 mg/g). These items, plus inconsistent sorbent-dose descriptions, require checking against the source figures/ESI. Units shown for humic-acid/microplastic additions and the thermodynamic table values also warrant verification. No product yield, quantified preparation cost, metal-leaching test or LCA is reported in the supplied main paper.

## B. Delmond et al. (2023) — timber waste for vancomycin removal
*Water, Air, and Soil Pollution* 234:81. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11270-023-06070-9. Existing candidate **CORP-006**. Primary study, including column/filtration tests and batch isotherms.

### Preparation and material properties
Sawdust was ground and sieved to 210–300 µm, washed five times with distilled water and dried at 90 °C for 20 h. Modified sawdust was treated sequentially using 10 wt% H3PO4, 20 wt% H2SO4 and 2.8 wt% KOH in ethanol, with 4 h mixing at room temperature and 2 h at 60 °C for each treatment, then washed to neutral pH and dried at 60 °C for 24 h. BET surface area increased from 0.7052 to 2.0451 m²/g for ash sawdust and from 0.8261 to 2.1827 m²/g for mixed sawdust.

### Fixed-bed/filtration results
The main comparison used 100 mL vancomycin solution at **100 mg/L**, 2 g adsorbent, 100 min contact/filtration and 20 ± 1 °C. Removal was:
- Modified mixed sawdust: **63.0 ± 2.3%**
- Modified ash sawdust: **56.6 ± 5.1%**
- Powdered activated carbon control: **98.2 ± 2.9%**
- Unmodified sawdust: below 15%

At a 2 g load, reported capacities were 3.11 mg/g (modified mixed), 2.79 mg/g (modified ash), and 9.33 mg/g (activated carbon). At 1 g, the corresponding capacities were 4.76, 3.58 and 9.23 mg/g. Increasing sawdust dose raised percent removal while reducing uptake per gram.

### Isotherm anomaly and transferability
The isotherm tests used high-concentration model solutions (30–150 mg/L). Table 3 reports Langmuir qmax = **12,179.5 mg/g** for modified ash sawdust, which is irreconcilable with observed capacities around 2.79–3.58 mg/g in the main comparison and roughly up to 4 mg/g in plotted batch results. Treat this fitted qmax as unreliable unless independently resolved. The paper's equation/table may contain a fitting or transcription problem.

The article estimates that 1 g modified ash could treat about 330 L at 0.0085 mg/L. This is an extrapolation from high-concentration tests, not experimentally demonstrated low-concentration treatment. Sawdust heating values were 15.5–18.5 MJ/kg and the authors suggest thermal conversion for energy, but the end-of-life route was not validated for contaminant fate. No numerical cost comparison, chemical-use inventory, regeneration study, actual wastewater test or LCA was performed. AC costs cited in the introduction come from external literature rather than the paper's own costing.

## C. Shaha et al. (2025) — sulfonated graphene oxide for ciprofloxacin
*ACS Omega* 10 (2025), 40817–40832. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.4c11231. Existing candidate **CORP-024**. Primary experimental study with DFT support.

### Preparation and adsorption conditions
SGO was prepared from 3 g graphite by a modified Hummers-type procedure using a 9:1 concentrated H2SO4/H3PO4 mixture and 18 g KMnO4 at 30 °C for 24 h; the product was quenched, washed with 7.7 mol/L HCl, water, ethanol and diethyl ether, and dried at 60 °C. For a core batch test, 10 mg SGO was contacted with 50 mL of 50 µmol/L ciprofloxacin at pH 4.0, 150 rpm and 240 min. The authors explored 10–100 µmol/L initial concentration, 0.2 g/L SGO and 25–40 °C tests.

### Observed uptake vs fitted maximum
Table 1 reports qe(exp) of **45.45, 121.06, 237.12, 326.06 and 402.58 µmol/g** at C0 of 10, 25, 50, 75 and 100 µmol/L, respectively. The reuse section gives 239.39 µmol/g for the initial 50 µmol/L condition rather than 237.12 µmol/g in Table 1. Fitted Langmuir qm is **1000 µmol/g (331.35 mg/g)** and R² = 0.9998 across the reported 25–40 °C range.

This fitted qm is not an observed capacity. If the same 0.2 g/L dose and maximum C0 of 100 µmol/L apply to the isotherm, the simple mass-balance ceiling at that C0 is 500 µmol/g, so the fitted qm is an extrapolation beyond the directly observed range and requires verification of dose/fit setup and supplementary information. Text also reports 96.53% removal at 25 °C at the 50 µmol/L condition, while the Table 1 qe value implies a slightly different percentage under the stated dose; preserve the discrepancy.

### Mechanisms and regeneration
Authors report pseudo-second-order kinetics and Langmuir isotherm fit, with FTIR, SEM/EDX and DFT used to support electrostatic interactions, hydrogen bonding and π–π interactions. Uptake increases with initial concentration; percent removal declines from 97.32% at 10 µmol/L to 81.28% at 100 µmol/L. Coexisting ions reduced uptake/removal in the order Ca2+ > Mg2+ > K+ > Na+ at 0.1 M. Five adsorption/desorption cycles retained approximately **95.8%** of the reported initial uptake (239.39 to 229.29 µmol/g). Desorption used 1 M HCl in DMF; authors report 98.11% release within 10 min and over 99.14% within 240 min.

### Practicality limits
This is batch testing in prepared aqueous solutions, not real wastewater. SGO synthesis uses concentrated acids, KMnO4, HCl and organic solvents; regeneration uses acidified DMF. No product yield, solvent recovery, chemical/washwater inventory, actual process cost or LCA is reported. The article states results as duplicate measurements. Strong lab-scale removal and reuse performance therefore do not alone establish low environmental burden or industrial sustainability.
