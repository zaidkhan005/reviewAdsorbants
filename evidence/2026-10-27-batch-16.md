## A. Míguez-González et al. (2023) — adsorption of antibiotics on forestry/agro-food bio-adsorbents
*Environmental Research* 233 (2023), 116360. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envres.2023.116360. Existing candidate **CORP-017**. Primary experimental study.

### Materials and design
The study tested pine bark, oak ash from oak-log combustion, and mussel shell. The single-solute batch experiments used 0.5 g sorbent with 10 mL 0.005 M CaCl2, 25 °C, 48 h, triplicates and seven added concentrations from 0 to 600 µmol/L. The papers' supplementary Table S1 is referenced for full sorbent physicochemical values, but that supplement was not included in the supplied files.

### Main observations (Fig. 1, PDF p. 3)
- **Amoxicillin (AMX):** oak ash retained close to 100% across the tested concentrations; pine bark and mussel shell generally retained less than 20%.
- **Ciprofloxacin (CIP):** pine bark retained 100% at all tested concentrations (so no isotherm fit was possible); oak ash and mussel shell approached about 70% and 80%, respectively, at high added concentration.
- **Trimethoprim (TMP):** pine bark retained nearly 100%; oak ash retention fell to below 30% at the highest added concentrations, and mussel shell was generally below 20%.

### Modelling and mass-balance caveat
Table 1 (PDF p. 5) gives AMX/oak-ash fitted Langmuir qmax = 14,909 µmol/kg (reported error 4,359; R²=0.93) and TMP/pine-bark qmax = 76,429 µmol/kg (reported error about 4.6×10⁴; R²=0.99). The study's own maximum loading point is 600 µmol/L × 0.010 L = 6 µmol added to 0.0005 kg sorbent, i.e. a simple maximum uptake of **12,000 µmol/kg** if all of it is retained. The fitted TMP/pine-bark qmax is therefore much higher than the experimentally accessible loading and has a very large error. The paper itself notes that saturation may not have been reached and some Langmuir parameters have high errors. Do not use these fitted qmax values as validated observed capacities or direct capacity rankings.

### Desorption and interpretation
The desorption step used fresh 0.005 M CaCl2 for 48 h at 25 °C. AMX release was near zero from oak ash but reached around 80% from mussel shell and 60% from pine bark at selected additions. CIP release was below 10% at the two highest concentrations. TMP release from pine bark was very low (reported maximum 6.29% at 25 µmol/L; described as <5% across the discussion), while mussel shell could release nearly all retained TMP.

Authors associate oak ash's high Ca and non-crystalline Fe/Al plus alkaline pH with cationic bridging for AMX, and pine bark's carboxyl/hydroxyl groups with hydrogen bonding for CIP/TMP. These remain mechanistic interpretations. The proposed application is retaining antibiotics in agricultural soils to limit migration into water/food chains; there is no column treatment trial, real wastewater validation, cost inventory or LCA in the main paper.

## B. Cuevas et al. (2019) — mineral materials for tetracycline sorption
*Minerals* 9 (2019), 453. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/min9070453. Existing candidate **DISC-016**. Primary experimental study.

### Materials and processing continuum
The paper compares natural clays (sepiolite, stevensite, illite, beidellite and low-/high-charge montmorillonites) with synthetic ferrihydrite/goethite and calcined sewage sludge. Ferrihydrite/goethite require precipitation, aging, washing and freeze-drying; the sludge-derived residue was calcined at 800 °C. Natural clays were largely tested without purification to represent realistic mineral products. These are not all equivalent in processing burden.

### Experimental conditions and observed uptake
Triplicate batch isotherms were run at 25 °C, pH 4/6/8, 0.1 M NaCl and initial TC concentrations 33.2, 131.6, 322.6, 625.0 and 1176.5 mg/L. Figure 7 and the discussion (PDF pp. 12–13) report observed uptake for low-charge montmorillonite (LC Mnt) of 13.2, 51.2, 125.5, 242 and 381.2 mg/g at the five concentrations, respectively. At the highest concentration, high-charge montmorillonite with low Fe content reached 342.14 mg/g. Ferrihydrite and illite reached approximately 8–10 mg/g at the lowest concentration near pH 8, with uptake patterns different from the higher-capacity smectites. The authors conclude LC Mnt has the highest uptake over the tested pH range, plausibly because its less tightly stacked layers expose more internal surface.

### Important transferability and model caveats
The lowest experimental concentration (33.2 mg/L = 33,200 µg/L) is still far above the paper's own cited typical treated-effluent concentration (<1 µg/L). Therefore, the high observed capacities, including 381.2 mg/g, come from high-load laboratory experiments and do not demonstrate trace-level effluent treatment.

Tables 3–4 (PDF pp. 14–15) report Freundlich parameters and Langmuir K/Cm. The Langmuir **Cm unit is cmol/kg**, not mg/g; it must not be copied into a qmax table without justified conversion. Some values are substantially above listed CEC (e.g. sepiolite Cm 392/160/125 cmol/kg vs CEC 20 cmol/kg), and the authors discuss near-linear/anomalous fit behavior. High R² alone is not enough to choose the better model.

The PZSE/material-charge discussion (PDF pp. 9–13) links observed pH effects to mineral surface charge, layer charge, CEC and TC speciation. The study does not report real wastewater trials, regeneration, fixed-bed results, reagent/energy inventories per mass of sorbent, process cost or life-cycle analysis.

## C. Abd El-Monaem et al. (2022) — chitosan-composite antibiotic adsorption review
*Arabian Journal of Chemistry* 15 (2022), 103743. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arabjc.2022.103743. Existing candidate **CORP-001**. Review article; supporting source only.

### Review scope and taxonomy
The review surveys chitosan composites across 11 antibiotic families, including tetracycline, ciprofloxacin, amoxicillin, levofloxacin, ceftriaxone, erythromycin, norfloxacin, ofloxacin, doxycycline, cefotaxime and sulfamethoxazole. It discusses magnetic oxides, biochar/carbon, graphene oxide, clays, silica, MOFs, hydrogels and grafted/crosslinked chitosan. Its discussion of chitosan production (Fig. 2, PDF p. 4) and modification routes helps map the processing continuum: unmodified chitosan has limitations including low solubility/stability, which motivate chemical or physical modifications.

### Review-reported secondary values
Table 2 (PDF p. 5) compiles tetracycline maxima including chitosan-g-AMPS (806.60 mg/g at pH 3), MMICs-Fe3+ (516.29 mg/g at pH 7–8), MOF-Cs (495.04 mg/g at pH 8–9), MSCsG (183.47 mg/g at pH 6), and Cs-olive pomace (1.6 mg/g at pH 8). Table 3 (PDF p. 9) compiles ciprofloxacin maxima including MgO/Cs/GO (1111 mg/g at pH 7), MCsGO (282.9 mg/g at pH 4–9), Fe-CS NCs (142.85 mg/g at pH 6), and Cs/BC (>76 mg/g at pH 3). These are secondary values from different cited primary studies, with differing concentrations, doses and fitting conventions. They are discovery pointers, not a harmonized comparison.

The AMX section (PDF p. 11) summarizes a chitosan-stabilized Fe/Ni composite with AMX removal of 93% in a single solution but 68.9% in AMX/Cd²⁺ binary solution. The mechanism as described in the review includes Fe corrosion, hydrogen radicals and cleavage of the AMX β-lactam bond. Therefore, the reported endpoint may include reductive degradation and must not be classified as adsorption alone without inspecting the cited original Weng et al. (2013) paper.

### Appropriate role in our manuscript
Use this review for taxonomy, terminology, and finding primary papers. Before using any listed qmax, pH optimum or removal figure in the results synthesis, trace it to the original study, verify its units and test conditions, check the mass balance, and distinguish adsorption from catalytic/reductive transformation. The review itself provides no independent primary experiment, harmonized process-cost analysis or LCA.
